## 4. The headline finding — and why it is NOT a discovered edge

`v0.always_short` is **ROBUST** on the bear window (Sharpe 4.62, +56%, 7% drawdown) —
the **first non-Fragile arm in the entire project**. This is genuine and reproducible,
but it is **the in-window bootstrap's blind spot, not alpha**:

- `always_short` is a **perpetual directional bet** (short the whole window). On a
  sustained −56% downtrend its equity rises smoothly, so the moving-block bootstrap —
  which resamples *within* this window's returns — sees consistently positive blocks →
  p5 Sharpe ≫ 0 → ROBUST. The gate tests robustness-**to-resampling-within-a-window**,
  not robustness-**across-regimes**.
- It is the exact mirror of buy-and-hold: on the **bull** window `always_short` is
  FRAGILE and loses −47%; buy-and-hold there posts Sharpe 1.486 / +47% (and is only
  Fragile by its 62% drawdown tail). Each perpetual bet "works" only on the window
  that trends its way.
- You would need to **know the regime in advance** to pick `always_short` — that is
  market-timing the regime, the one thing the robustness program concluded you cannot
  do. So this is not a usable edge; it is a directional gamble that matched the window.

**The 4 short-TIMING strategies — the ones that try to *detect when* to short
(`sma_cross_ls`, `macd_ls`, `rsi_ls`, `bbands_ls`) — are ALL FRAGILE on both windows.**
Short timing fails exactly like long timing. That is the real, honest result: active
TA — long, short, or combined — does not produce a robust edge.

**Product consequence:** `always_short` lives in the separate `default_short_field`,
NOT the default 13-arm advisor field; the standard advisor never crowns it. A
perpetual directional arm that "passes" the gate on a matching window is precisely why
the benchmark is buy-and-hold (the long-bias default for crypto's long-run up-drift)
and why a regime-matched short is not auto-recommended. (A future relative-robustness
or walk-forward gate could close this blind spot — backlog.)
