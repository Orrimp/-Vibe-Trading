## Step 2 — the bug site

The defect is bug-log **#94**, inside the portfolio sizer: it sized a *resize* to the target rather
than to the delta. The sizer is `size_portfolio_target` at
[`crates/risk/src/portfolio.rs:67`](../../crates/risk/src/portfolio.rs); the production call site is
[`crates/backtest/src/scenarios/montecarlo.rs:520`](../../crates/backtest/src/scenarios/montecarlo.rs)
inside `run_path`, which is the lane behind all 34 surfaces.

Until `723ca742` (2026-08-23) that call site did not exist — `scripts/callers.sh
size_portfolio_target` found **zero** production callers where the bug-log census looked. So the
gross exposure cap and the drift band were both declared and neither bound, and #94 rode inside the
wiring that made them bind.

**What it did to the evidence.** The signature is unmistakable and corpus-wide: `p95_maxdd` improved
in **156 of 156** cells, median **85.53 % → 26.70 %**. The superseded corpus contains cells at
**100.00 %** tail drawdown — total account destruction — which no strategy in the grid actually
produces. Those were the sizer.

## Step 3 — the would-have-caught test, RED before the fix

[`crates/backtest/tests/portfolio_controls_bind.rs`](../../crates/backtest/tests/portfolio_controls_bind.rs)
builds a signed target vector per rebalance boundary and asserts that *both* controls bind. It was
RED-proven against the pre-fix code in `723ca742` before the fix landed — the commit message records
that the wiring "immediately found a defect that had made the drift band unenforceable". That is the
gate this class of bug now has to get past.

Two further gates were added by this re-lock rather than relying on the above:

- [`crates/backtest/tests/relock_manifest.rs`](../../crates/backtest/tests/relock_manifest.rs) derives
  all 34 invocations from `GridKind`, so the manifest cannot silently drift from the grid.
- Reproducibility was **measured, not assumed**: four surfaces across three slugs re-run in a separate
  process ~25 min later, all four body-SHAs byte-identical. And 0 of 34 bodies carry a wall-clock,
  pid, host or timestamp below the front-matter, so re-emitting them cannot reproduce bug-log `#106`.

## Step 4 — sign-off and the in-place landing

Operator ruling, 2026-09-25: **follow D6.b as ratified.** The alternative on the table was a D6.c
amendment legitimising a re-lock namespace with mandatory back-pointers; it was declined in favour of
one corpus.

The 34 new bodies were copied into the *same* anchored directory as the report each supersedes — 7
directories, matching the 7 namespaces the resolver already hardcodes. `verify_anchors.sh` picks the
newest matching file per namespace, so the new body is the one under gate and the superseded body
stays on disk beside it, plainly dated.

Every re-emitted row's comment block now carries the new headline numbers, the family verdict with
its delta, **the buy-and-hold control alongside it**, and the superseded numbers kept verbatim
underneath with their old SHA — so the correction is legible next to what it corrects, and nobody
has to take the correction on trust.

## Narrative re-statement (second operator ruling, same day)

Six 2023 surfaces across three slugs left `FAMILY-UNIFORM-FRAGILE`. The operator ruled to re-state
now, with the buy-and-hold dominance stated alongside, rather than wait for the C5 deflation pass.

That re-statement is carried in the anchor comment blocks above and in the errata. It is **not** a
thesis change and does not touch the era-qualified claim: **0 of 156 cells beat their own

`momentum-parameter-robustness-sweep` (held).

The honest form of the new sentence is stronger than the old one: these arms are not dismissed
because their numbers are noise. They trade, they produce real distributions, and they still lose to
doing nothing.
