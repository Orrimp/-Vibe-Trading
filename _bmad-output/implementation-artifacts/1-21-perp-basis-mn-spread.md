**VERDICT: FAIL.** Not because the code is broken — it builds, runs, and its FRAGILE verdicts are arithmetically real — but because **the evidence does not support the claims made from it.** The delivered surfaces answer a different question than the one the story asked.

- [x] [Review][Decision→1-25] **CRITICAL C1 (bug-log #75) — the MN basis arm never saw the basis; the pre-registered k2 criterion fired on funding compared with itself.** `MomentumStrategy` uses ONE field, `funding_map`, for two semantically different jobs — the SCORE sidecar and the ACCRUAL sidecar. The MN lane needs both, so the driver injects basis via `.with_funding()` and passes funding as `funding_override`; `run_path` then does `with_funding(Some(override))`, a full replacement. For MN, `funding_override` is ALWAYS `Some` (the short-leg accrual requires it), so the basis map is always discarded. And `basis_reversal_score` / `carry_score` are the same function modulo comments — same field, same shared rings, both `−mean` — so fed the same map they are bit-identical. **Derived independently by all three layers; re-verified at source by the orchestrator; settled by a CONTROL**: `mn-basisperp`, whose basis rides a *different* field (`basis_score_map`), differs from `mn-funding` in every number (p50 −0.064156 vs +0.013327, liquidations 328 vs 148), while `mn-basis` differs in **none** — the bodies differ only in title, `score_source=` label, and one prose word. Basis and funding correlate ~+0.47/+0.66, not +1.0; two real series cannot coincide to six decimals across 200 paths including identical integer liquidation counts. **It also broke a documented invariant**: D-BR.3's safety argument is a precondition on a SIBLING field ("the accrual stays gated `None` for the basis arm — D-BR.1"). This story is the first to set that field while scoring `BasisReversal`, and nobody re-derived the argument.
- [x] [Review][Decision→1-25] **CRITICAL C2 (bug-log #76) — the residual arm ranks the basis axis INVERTED against its own spec.** Rank 1 = highest score = LOWEST basis; `residual = rank(basis) − rank(funding)`; `top_k_long` takes the HIGHEST residual ⇒ the arm longs the **highest** basis, while its doc (and `config.rs`) say "Long = highest residual (low-basis relative to its funding level)". Orchestrator-verified through `selector.rs` and a worked example on the story's own fixture. Every residual test asserts *difference* or *inequality*; **none pins direction** — and the inverse satisfies "differs from the raw basis arm" exactly as well as the intended construction. The nearest test bakes the confusion in: its assertion message says "AA has high basis" while AA's basis is the lowest in the fixture.
- [x] [Review][Decision→1-25] **The joint consequence, which is the real headline: NO anchored MN surface tested the basis in its documented direction.** #108-#111 never loaded it (C1), #112-#115 are funding by design, #116-#119 loaded it backwards (C2). Therefore the recorded reading — *"basis ≡ funding byte-identically → basis IS the funding mirror → the derivatives-positioning domain is CLOSED with finality"* — **is not supported by this evidence.** The FRAGILE verdicts themselves stand; the causal reading of *why* does not. **Stated with the discipline the thesis requires**: a negative median from an inverted arm is *consistent with* an edge in the intended direction, which is **NOT** a claim that one exists — the #73 ~10× funding over-accrual, the liquidation regime, and ordinary noise all live in that same number. Honest status: **unknown pending a correctly-signed re-run.** CHANGELOG corrected and the closure SUSPENDED (commit `46dd71d`).

- [x] [Review][Adjudicated] **#71 — the layers disagreed and both were right about different paths.** Blind found the cap cannot bind because opens are sized at `0.10·equity` against a `0.40` cap; Edge and the Auditor found a *cover* order is sized at the **full short notional**, so as the short rises and equity falls `cover_notional/equity` crosses 0.40 and the close is silently rejected.
