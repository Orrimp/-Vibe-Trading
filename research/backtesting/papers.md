### [1] The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality
- **Authors / Venue:** David H. Bailey, Marcos López de Prado / Journal of Portfolio Management 40(5), pp. 94–107
- **Year:** 2014
- **Source:** SSRN:2460551 (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551); PDF https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf
- **% read:** 88% (FULL text + ALL THREE appendices read via pdftotext extraction of the davidhbailey.com PDF — main method, the EVT derivation sketch of E[max SR] (App.1), the Python verification snippet (App.2), and the dependent-trials N estimation (App.3); skipped only the heat-map figure internals)
- **Summary:** When you screen N strategy candidates and keep the best Sharpe ratio (SR), that max SR is upward-biased even if all strategies are pure noise — the expected maximum SR among N *independent* trials is centered well above zero and grows with N and shrinks with track-record length T. DSR = `PSR(SR0) = Z[ (SR̂ − SR0)·√(T−1) / √(1 − γ3·SR̂ + ((γ4−1)/4)·SR̂²) ]` (Eq.2), i.e. it deflates the standard PSR's benchmark from 0 to the selection-bias threshold `SR0 = E[max SR]`. The threshold (Eq.1, derived via Extreme Value Theory / Gumbel in App.1): `E[max SR] ≈ √V[SR̂]·[ (1−γ)·Z⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ·Z⁻¹(1−1/(N·e)) ]`, γ≈0.5772 (Euler–Mascheroni), e Euler's number, V[SR̂] = the **cross-trial variance of the Sharpe estimates** (NOT the sampling variance of one SR — a point easy to get wrong). App.2 ships exact Python (`getExpMaxSR`: `maxZ=(1-emc)*norm.ppf(1-1./N)+emc*norm.ppf(1-1./(N*e)); return mu+sigma*maxZ`). **Worked example (the load-bearing one):** an annualized SR̂=2.5 over T=1250 daily bars (5y), with γ3=−3, γ4=10, V[SR̂]=1, N=100 trials → `E[max SR]≈... ` deflates the result to **DSR=0.90** (only a 90% chance the true SR>0 — REJECTED at 95%); had N been only 46, DSR=0.9505 (would PASS); and had returns been Gaussian (γ3=0,γ4=3) it would take N=88 trials to fall to that same level — so **non-normality alone roughly halves the tolerable trial count**. **App.3 (the N-estimation crux):** the N in E[max SR] must be the number of *independent* trials; given M *dependent* trials, derive implied-independent `N̂` two ways: (a) **average-correlation interpolation** — `N̂ = ρ̄ + (1−ρ̄)·M` (Eq.9), where ρ̄ is the weighted-average off-diagonal correlation of the trial return matrix (ρ̄→1 ⇒ N̂→1; ρ̄→0 ⇒ N̂→M), optionally Fisher-transformed; (b) **information-theory** (entropy / total-correlation / multiinformation) counting non-redundant sources, which the authors prefer as "a much deeper concept of redundancy than correlation." Critical caveat they flag: when M > T (more trials than bars — our exact situation) the correlation matrix is ill-conditioned and ρ̄ is itself overfit → must first dimension-reduce the matrix. Also defines MinTRL (shortest T for significance given N) and ties to the secretary-problem 1/e-rule for *when to stop testing* (sample ~37% of theory-justified configs, then take the first that beats all prior).
- **Relevance to our system:** This is THE canonical multiple-testing correction and the centerpiece of our gate-add — and the full read sharpens three things the abstract glossed. (1) **V[SR̂] is the dispersion of Sharpes ACROSS our swept configs, not the SE of one** — our bake-off must record every config's Sharpe and compute their variance; a TIGHT cluster of config-Sharpes near hold's = tiny V[SR̂] = low E[max SR] (little to deflate, consistent with no-edge), while a wide spread inflates the bar. (2) **App.3 gives us the original effective-N estimator** ([86]'s ONC clustering is the later refinement): `N̂ = ρ̄ + (1−ρ̄)·M` is a one-line closed form we could ship immediately from the config return-correlation matrix, with the ENTROPY route as the rigorous upgrade — and the M>T ill-conditioning warning is decisive for us (a 2-year daily window has T≈500 bars but our sweep can exceed that many configs, so we MUST dimension-reduce / cluster before estimating ρ̄). (3) The worked example calibrates expectations: a genuinely spectacular SR̂=2.5/5yr is rejected at just N=100 trials once crypto's fat tails (γ4≈10) and negative skew (γ3≈−3) enter — so for our SMA/MACD crowns, which rarely net an SR near 2.5, DSR will almost always land in the reject region (our thesis). **Crown only if DSR>0.95 AND net return beats buy-and-hold; B&H is exempt (benchmark, not a searched trial).** Non-normality term BITES for crypto. Complements (does not replace) the bootstrap: bootstrap tests path-robustness of ONE strategy; DSR tests selection bias ACROSS the many we compared.

### [2] The Probability of Backtest Overfitting
- **Authors / Venue:** David H. Bailey, Jonathan M. Borwein, Marcos López de Prado, Qiji Jim Zhu / Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), 39–69
- **Year:** 2015 (rev.)
- **Source:** SSRN:2326253 (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253); PDF https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf
- **% read:** 80% (FULL text read via clean pdftotext of the davidhbailey.com PDF — the formal (IS,OOS) framework, the exact CSCV Algorithm 2.3 with the logit construction, and the four overfit statistics; skipped the numerical test cases and the EVT appendix)
- **Summary:** Defines Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) as the probability the strategy chosen best IN-SAMPLE ranks BELOW the median OUT-OF-SAMPLE — the IS optimum fitting noise. Exact definition (Eq. 2.2): `PBO = Σ_n Prob[r_n < N/2 | r ∈ Ω*_n]·Prob[r ∈ Ω*_n]`, where Ω*_n is the event that strategy n is IS-optimal. **CSCV Algorithm 2.3 (now precise from primary):** (1) build the T×N matrix M of per-config P&L series (rows synchronous across configs); (2) split the T rows into **S disjoint equal time-blocks**; (3) form all **C(S, S/2)** combinations choosing half the blocks as the training/IS set J, complement as testing/OOS set J̄ (each combination's IS half is re-used as another's OOS half — the "symmetric" part); (4) in each combination find the IS-best config n* (`r_{n*} ∈ Ω*`), compute its OOS **relative rank `ω̄ = r̄_{n*}/(N+1) ∈ (0,1)`**, then the **logit `λ = ln(ω̄/(1−ω̄))`** (high λ = IS-OOS consistency = low overfit); (5) **PBO = fraction of combinations with λ < 0** (the IS-winner landed OOS-below-median). Model-free, nonparametric, needs ONLY the per-config return series, no knowledge of the underlying models — explicitly note this measures overfitting of the SELECTION PROCESS, not model calibration. Important nuance from the full read: the IS/OOS ORDER doesn't matter for the Sharpe ratio but DOES matter for path-dependent metrics like return/max-drawdown ratio (so block ordering must be preserved for those). The framework yields four statistics: PBO, **performance degradation** (OOS-vs-IS slope — captures the "selecting better IS gives WORSE OOS" pathology), probability of loss OOS, and stochastic-dominance. Key polemic: hold-out is unreliable (future-knowledge leakage; high variance; fails for short samples), and a 5% false-positive rate holds for ONE test only — a large config sweep makes false positives near-certain.
- **Relevance to our system:** Highly actionable and a near-perfect fit for our bake-off. CSCV needs ONLY the per-strategy return/equity series we already produce for every candidate in the sweep, so we could add a PBO number to the ranking output with no new modeling. It would quantify the chance our crowned strategy is an IS artifact — exactly the failure mode behind our validated "nothing robustly beats holding" thesis. The "performance degradation" slope (how much OOS Sharpe drops vs IS) and "probability of loss OOS" are cheap, interpretable companions to our bootstrap verdict. Note CSCV's combinatorial IS/OOS swapping is conceptually adjacent to combinatorial purged CV (Lopez de Prado's later book) — for our single-coin time series we'd want PURGING/embargo between IS and OOS blocks to avoid leakage across the split boundary. Also reinforces a reporting discipline: we should record and surface N (number of configs tried) so the operator can judge overfit risk.

### [3] Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism: The Effects of Backtest Overfitting on Out-of-Sample Performance
- **Authors / Venue:** David H. Bailey, Jonathan M. Borwein, Marcos López de Prado, Qiji Jim Zhu / Notices of the American Mathematical Society 61(5), pp. 458–471
- **Year:** 2014
- **Source:** SSRN:2308659 (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2308659); PDF https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-pseudo.pdf (scanned — read via OCR)
- **% read:** 75% (FULL text re-read via clean pdftotext of the davidhbailey.com PDF — the MinBTL Theorem 3.1, the model-complexity and compensation-effects sections, and the precise N–T calibrations; skipped figure internals and the fraud worked example)
- **Summary:** The flagship polemic, now read from a clean extraction. Proves high in-sample Sharpe is trivially achievable by trying a modest number of configs on a FIXED dataset. **MinBTL Theorem 3.1** (exact): the backtest length in years needed to avoid selecting a skill-less strategy (expected OOS Sharpe = 0) whose IS Sharpe reaches E[maxN] among N independent trials is `MinBTL ≈ [ (1−γ)Z⁻¹(1−1/N) + γZ⁻¹(1−1/(N·e⁻¹)) ]² / E[maxN]² < 2·ln[N] / E[maxN]²` — the clean upper bound is **`MinBTL < 2 ln[N] / E[maxN]²`**, growing as ~2 ln N in the trial count. The load-bearing calibrations (E[maxN] fixed at 1): with only **5 years of data, no more than 45 independent configs** may be tried or you are "almost guaranteed" a strategy with IS Sharpe 1 but OOS Sharpe 0; and after just **7 independent configs the expected max IS Sharpe is 1 on a 2-year backtest** with OOS Sharpe 0 — so an SR≈1 over 2 years is consistent with PURE noise once you tried ~7 configs. Critical caveat (stated in the primary): Proposition 2.1 assumes INDEPENDENT trials (conservative); for DEPENDENT trials, derive the effective N via a **dimension-reduction procedure such as Principal Component Analysis** (a third N-estimation route alongside [1]-App.3's average-correlation and entropy, and [86]'s ONC clustering). MinBTL is explicitly a NECESSARY-not-sufficient condition (a backtest longer than MinBTL can still be overfit). Key economics result: WITHOUT memory/compensation effects, overfitting yields ~zero OOS; WITH serial dependence / "compensation effects" (overcrowding, mean reversion), overfitting induces strictly NEGATIVE OOS performance — overfit strategies actively lose. The blunt closer: a backtest that does not report N "makes it impossible to assess the risk of overfitting."
- **Relevance to our system:** The intellectual foundation of our entire product thesis ("no active strategy robustly beats holding, net of costs"). MinBTL is a concrete guardrail: given the number of configs our bake-off sweeps (N), MinBTL tells us the MINIMUM history length the (coin,window) must have before any crowned IS-Sharpe is even potentially meaningful — we could refuse to crown / flag low-confidence when window_years < 2 ln(N)/target². The negative-OOS-under-memory result is a strong argument for WHY our forward paper-trade so often underperforms buy-and-hold for the crowned non-trivial strategies. Directly motivates surfacing N and a MinBTL check in the ranking UI. Pairs with [1] (DSR) and [2] (PBO) as the three-part anti-overfitting toolkit we should consider porting.

### [4] The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier
- **Authors / Venue:** David H. Bailey, Marcos López de Prado / Journal of Risk 15(2), pp. 3–44
- **Year:** 2012
- **Source:** SSRN:1821643; PDF https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/sharpe-frontier.pdf
- **% read:** 30% (abstract + Probabilistic/Minimum Track Record Length sections; via prior knowledge of this paper confirmed against fetched directory listing)
- **Summary:** Introduces the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (PSR) and Minimum Track Record Length (MinTRL) BEFORE the multiple-testing extensions. PSR(SR*) = probability that the true SR exceeds a benchmark SR*, computed as Z[ (SR−SR*)·√(T−1) / √(1 − skew·SR + ((kurt−1)/4)·SR²) ] — i.e. it converts an observed SR into a confidence level while correcting for skewness, kurtosis and sample length. MinTRL is the smallest T such that PSR exceeds a chosen confidence (e.g. 95%) for SR*=0: MinTRL = 1 + [1 − skew·SR + ((kurt−1)/4)·SR²]·(Z_conf / SR)². Defines the "Sharpe ratio efficient frontier" — the set of portfolios maximizing PSR rather than SR — and shows that maximizing SR ≠ maximizing the PROBABILITY that SR is real, because higher moments matter.
- **Relevance to our system:** PSR/MinTRL are the single-strategy building blocks the later DSR/MinBTL papers generalize to the multiple-testing case. For our app, PSR is the right way to report confidence in a single crowned strategy's Sharpe on the forward window (correcting for crypto's fat tails/skew), and MinTRL answers "how long must the paper-trade run before its Sharpe is trustworthy?" Cheap to compute from the equity curve we already store. Good candidate for the per-strategy confidence badge in the ranking output.

### [5] . . . and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns
- **Authors / Venue:** Campbell R. Harvey, Yan Liu, Heqing Zhu / Review of Financial Studies 29(1), pp. 5–68; NBER WP 20592
- **Year:** 2016
- **Source:** SSRN:2249314 (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2249314); PDF https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20592/w20592.pdf
- **% read:** 55% (abstract, intro, full multiple-testing methodology section, recommended-threshold results via PDF text extraction; skipped per-factor tables)
- **Summary:** Catalogs 316 published "factors" purporting to explain the cross-section of returns and argues most are likely false discoveries from data mining. Because the literature collectively ran a huge number of tests, the conventional t-ratio > 2.0 significance bar is far too lax. Applies three multiple-testing corrections: Bonferroni and Holm (both control the Family-Wise Error Rate, FWER = P(≥1 false positive)) and Benjamini–Hochberg–Yekutieli (BHY, which controls the False Discovery Rate, FDR = expected proportion of false positives among rejections; less stringent — FDR ≤ FWER). Recommended hurdles: Bonferroni t-ratio rises from 1.96 (1967) to 3.78 by 2012 (→4.0 by 2032); Holm similar; BHY stabilizes at ~3.39 (1% FDR) / 3.18 (5% FDR). Bottom line: a NEW factor needs a t-ratio of at least ~3.0 (p≈0.27%), and they argue the practical minimum is ~3.18. They also note 316 understates the true number of trials (file-drawer/unpublished tests), so even these thresholds are optimistic; suggest haircutting toward ~113 "common" factors.
- **Relevance to our system:** The asset-pricing analogue of our bake-off's multiple-testing problem. Our sweep tests many strategy configs on one series and crowns a winner — the same "factor zoo" data-mining setup. The core lesson is directly portable: the t-stat / Sharpe significance bar must RISE with the number of configs tried. The FWER-vs-FDR distinction gives us a design choice for the ranking gate — FWER (Bonferroni/Holm) if we want near-zero tolerance for crowning a false winner (conservative, fits our "nothing beats holding" prior), or BHY/FDR if we tolerate a controlled false-discovery proportion across many (coin,window) runs. The file-drawer point reinforces honest accounting of N (every config in the sweep, not just the survivors). Concretely: convert our crowned strategy's Sharpe to a t-stat (t ≈ SR·√(years)·√(periods/yr)) and require it to clear a Bonferroni/BHY hurdle for the sweep's N before declaring a "beat."

### [6] A Reality Check for Data Snooping
- **Authors / Venue:** Halbert White / Econometrica 68(5), pp. 1097–1126
- **Year:** 2000
- **Source:** DOI:10.1111/1468-0262.00152 (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1468-0262.00152); summarized via Corradi-Swanson survey + abstract (primary PDF blocked by TLS/cert error)
- **% read:** 35% (abstract + methodology via secondary survey sources; could not open primary PDF)
- **Summary:** The foundational data-snooping test. When you search over l candidate models/rules and report the best one's performance vs a benchmark, the best's apparent superiority is upward-biased by the search itself. White's Reality Check (RC) tests the single null H0: "the BEST model among all l has NO predictive superiority over the benchmark," i.e. max_k E[f_k] ≤ 0 where f_k is model k's performance differential vs the benchmark. The test statistic is V = max_k √n · f̄_k (the max over models of the standardized average differential). Its sampling distribution under the null is obtained by the stationary bootstrap (Politis–Romano) — resample blocks of the differential series, recompute the max statistic many times, and the RC p-value is the fraction of bootstrap maxima exceeding the observed V. This automatically accounts for the FULL universe of rules tried, so a "great" best rule that's really noise gets a high (insignificant) p-value.
- **Relevance to our system:** This is essentially the academic ancestor of what our app already does — a bootstrap test of whether the BEST strategy genuinely beats a benchmark, where OUR benchmark is buy-and-hold. The mapping is almost 1:1: f_k = (strategy k return − buy&hold return); test whether max_k beats 0 via block bootstrap. We should verify our gate's null is framed as "best beats benchmark accounting for all configs tried" (RC-style) rather than "this one strategy beats benchmark" — the former is the multiple-testing-correct version. The stationary/moving-block bootstrap White uses is the SAME family as our moving-block bootstrap, validating our core method choice. Key caveat carried into [7]: RC is CONSERVATIVE when the search includes many bad rules.

### [7] A Test for Superior Predictive Ability (SPA)
- **Authors / Venue:** Peter Reinhard Hansen / Journal of Business & Economic Statistics 23(4), pp. 365–380
- **Year:** 2005
- **Source:** SSRN:264569 (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=264569 — 403 gated); summarized via search abstracts + Corradi-Swanson survey
- **% read:** 30% (abstract + methodology summary from multiple secondary sources; primary gated)
- **Summary:** Improves White's Reality Check on two fronts. (1) STUDENTIZATION: instead of the raw max √n·f̄_k, SPA uses a studentized statistic max_k √n·f̄_k / σ̂_k, which down-weights erratic/high-variance rules so a single noisy rule can't dominate the max. (2) SAMPLE-DEPENDENT NULL: White's RC builds the null under the "least favorable configuration" (all models exactly at the boundary E[f_k]=0), which is overly conservative when many genuinely-poor models (E[f_k]≪0) are in the set — they shouldn't count toward the snooping penalty. SPA instead estimates which models are "relevant" (near the boundary) and excludes obviously-poor ones from the null distribution, recovering power. Result: SPA is more powerful and far less sensitive to the inclusion of irrelevant bad rules than RC, while still controlling size. Empirically validated (Hansen & Lunde, Hsu & Kuan) as more powerful than RC.
- **Relevance to our system:** Directly relevant if our bake-off sweeps a wide config grid that includes many obviously-bad strategies (e.g. degenerate SMA windows). Under a White-style gate, those bad configs inflate the snooping penalty and make it HARDER for a genuinely-good strategy to register as beating buy-and-hold — i.e. our gate could be too conservative. Adopting SPA-style studentization (divide each strategy's excess-return by its bootstrap std) and excluding clearly-dominated configs from the null would give our gate more power to detect a real winner if one exists, WITHOUT inflating false positives. Worth checking whether our current 1000-path gate is RC-style (conservative) or SPA-style; if RC-style, SPA is a power upgrade.

### [8] Stepwise Multiple Testing as Formalized Data Snooping (StepM)
- **Authors / Venue:** Joseph P. Romano, Michael Wolf / Econometrica 73(4), pp. 1237–1282
- **Year:** 2005
- **Source:** DOI:10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00615.x; PDF https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~behansen/718/RomanoWolf2005.pdf
- **% read:** 70% (FULL text read via pdftotext of Hansen's Wisconsin mirror PDF — the basic vs STUDENTIZED statistic, the step-down algorithm, the FWE-control argument, and Section 7 on bootstrap block-size choice for time series; skipped the asymptotic consistency proofs)
- **Summary:** Generalizes White's RC from "does the single best model beat the benchmark?" to "identify ALL models that beat the benchmark" while controlling FWE = P(≥1 false rejection). The StepM algorithm: Step 1 — compute the max statistic over all remaining hypotheses, get a bootstrap critical value (resampling to capture the JOINT dependence of test statistics), reject every hypothesis exceeding it; Step 2 — remove the rejected ones, recompute the max critical value over the SURVIVORS; repeat until no further rejections. Because the critical value SHRINKS as strong performers are peeled off, StepM rejects strictly more true-false hypotheses than single-step RC and far more than Bonferroni/Holm (which ignore dependence). Two refinements now from the full read. (1) **STUDENTIZATION (their explicit recommendation "when feasible"):** use `z_Ts = w_Ts / σ̂_Ts` (the basic statistic w divided by its estimated SE) rather than the raw w — this improves finite-sample level and power, the same idea Hansen's SPA [7] uses, computed with a **prewhitened kernel variance estimator** (QS kernel). (2) **Section 7 — a concrete block-size selector for the bootstrap (Algorithm 7.1, the "calibration function" method):** fit a SEMIPARAMETRIC model (they use a VAR(1)) to the data — chosen because such models have no block size of their own — simulate M datasets from it, and for each candidate block size b compute the empirical coverage `g(b)`; pick the b̃ minimizing `|g(b) − (1−α)|` (coverage closest to nominal). They use the CIRCULAR block bootstrap and report concrete choices b=20 (basic) / b=15 (studentized) in their application, noting the block-size choice is "even more crucial" for the studentized statistic. Still controls FWE asymptotically.
- **Relevance to our system:** If we ever surface not just THE single crowned strategy but the full SET of strategies that robustly beat buy-and-hold ("these 3 of 40 configs cleared the gate"), StepM is the principled FWE-controlling way — and it reuses our existing bootstrap machinery. Its dependence-aware critical values are more powerful than the Bonferroni hurdle of [5] because our configs are HIGHLY correlated (neighboring SMA windows produce near-identical equity); Bonferroni over-penalizes that correlation, StepM/bootstrap handles it correctly. Two now-concrete imports from the full read. (1) **STUDENTIZE our gate statistic** — divide each config's excess-return-vs-hold by its (prewhitened-kernel) bootstrap SE before taking the max, exactly as Romano-Wolf recommend and as SPA [7][93] does; this is the power upgrade that stops the many obviously-bad configs we sweep from burying a genuine winner, and the primary confirms it materially improves finite-sample level/power. (2) **Algorithm 7.1 gives us a SECOND, coverage-calibrated block-size selector** to pair with Politis-White [20]: fit a VAR(1) to the (coin,window) series, simulate, and pick the block size whose empirical coverage best matches nominal — directly usable, and the paper's note that block size is "even more crucial" for the studentized statistic means if we adopt (1) we MUST also do principled block sizing. Their concrete b=20/15 on a circular bootstrap is a sanity anchor for our own block length. Strong candidate for upgrading our ranking from "top-1" to "set of gate-passers" with rigorous multiplicity AND power handling.

### [9] Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV) + Purging/Embargo
- **Authors / Venue:** Marcos López de Prado / "Advances in Financial Machine Learning" (Wiley), ch. 7 & 12; summarized via Wikipedia "Purged cross-validation" + practitioner write-ups
- **Year:** 2018
- **Source:** Book ISBN 978-1119482086; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation ; https://www.quantbeckman.com/p/with-code-combinatorial-purged-cross
- **% read:** 45% (full method via Wikipedia + two practitioner explainers with code; not the book chapter verbatim)
- **Summary:** Two ideas that fix leakage in financial CV, plus a combinatorial scheme that yields a DISTRIBUTION of backtest paths. (1) PURGING: drop training observations whose LABEL-formation interval overlaps the test set's time range (financial labels depend on future returns, so naive K-fold leaks). (2) EMBARGO: additionally drop a small percentage of observations immediately AFTER each test fold (e.g. 1–5% / 50 of 1000 obs) to kill serial-correlation leakage that purging alone misses. (3) CPCV: split the series into N sequential non-overlapping groups, then use ALL combinations of k groups as the test set (C(N,k) splits), applying purge+embargo each time. Because each group appears in multiple test sets, you reconstruct φ[N,k] = (k/N)·C(N,k) distinct out-of-sample backtest PATHS — e.g. N=6,k=2 gives 15 splits → 5 paths. This converts a single point-estimate Sharpe into an empirical DISTRIBUTION of Sharpes, enabling percentile-based (e.g. 10th-pct PSR) selection — reframing optimization as ROBUSTNESS identification.
- **Relevance to our system:** CPCV is the gold-standard purged evaluator and is philosophically identical to what our gate is trying to do — produce a DISTRIBUTION of outcomes rather than one number and judge robustness by the downside (weakest-link). Two concrete imports: (a) PURGE + EMBARGO our IS/OOS / bootstrap block boundaries — even though our strategies use point-in-time signals (smaller leakage horizon than ML meta-labels), any indicator with a lookback window (SMA-200, Bollinger) creates feature-overlap at block edges that an embargo would clean. (b) The φ[N,k] combinatorial-path idea is an ALTERNATIVE/complement to our moving-block bootstrap for generating many OOS paths from limited history — both attack the same "one history = one lucky path" problem. Our weakest-link verdict ≈ CPCV's "select by a low percentile of the path distribution." This is probably the single most transferable methodology to our gate.

### [10] The Stationary Bootstrap
- **Authors / Venue:** Dimitris N. Politis, Joseph P. Romano / Journal of the American Statistical Association 89(428), pp. 1303–1313
- **Year:** 1994
- **Source:** DOI:10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870; mechanics via SAS/IML explainer + multiple secondary sources (primary tandfonline/semanticscholar not directly fetchable)
- **% read:** 35% (abstract + full mechanics from secondary explainers; not the JASA PDF verbatim)
- **Summary:** The canonical resampling method for weakly-dependent STATIONARY time series, the parent of the bootstrap used in White's RC [6]. Plain IID bootstrap destroys autocorrelation, so block methods resample contiguous chunks to preserve local dependence. The MOVING-BLOCK bootstrap uses FIXED-length blocks but the resulting series is non-stationary (block boundaries are special). The STATIONARY bootstrap fixes this by drawing each block's length from a GEOMETRIC distribution with parameter p (expected block length = 1/p): pick a random start, take a geometric-length run, concatenate until you reach the series length. Random block lengths make the resampled series itself stationary. The mean block length 1/p is the single tuning knob: small p (long blocks) preserves more serial correlation; p→1 collapses to the IID bootstrap. Wrap-around (circular) handling avoids end effects.
- **Relevance to our system:** This is the theoretical backbone of OUR gate. Our "1000-path moving-block bootstrap" is the FIXED-length sibling of this method; the key design parameter we share is the BLOCK LENGTH, which must be long enough to preserve crypto's autocorrelation/volatility-clustering or the bootstrap will understate risk and over-reward strategies. Two takeaways: (a) consider whether the stationary (random-length) variant would remove block-boundary artifacts our fixed-length version may have; (b) block length is not a free choice — there's an optimal-block-length literature (Politis-White 2004) we should consult to set ours principled rather than ad hoc. Confirms our method is squarely within accepted practice for dependent-data resampling.
