## Bottom line for the operator (read this first)

**No deep-learning or RL method in this literature is realistically deployable as the alpha engine of a single-coin paper advisor.** Three independent reasons:
1. **Where DL/RL genuinely adds value, it is cross-sectional and tiny.** The most rigorous study ([24] Gu-Kelly-Xiu, RFS) shows ML's real edge comes from nonlinear interactions across *many features and many assets*, and even then absolute out-of-sample R² is sub-1% per month. A single coin with a handful of features is the opposite of that setting.
2. **The forecasting edge of deep models over trivial baselines is small or nonexistent.** [8] (DLinear, AAAI-Oral) shows one-layer linear models beat Informer/Autoformer; [22] (PatchTST) only recovers a single-digit-% forecasting-error edge after careful design — and on physics/utility data, never cost-net trading P&L.
3. **The spectacular crypto-trading numbers do not survive scrutiny.** [10] 4x/50d (2017 bull + survivorship), [21] 6654%/yr (implausible 82% accuracy, leakage-smelling, costs not credible), [23] Sharpe 2.7 (30-day out-of-sample, no buy-and-hold compare), [28] +176% crypto SAC (no buy-and-hold benchmark), [56] CryptoMamba $100→$263 (no buy-and-hold on an uptrending year), [75] KASPER Sharpe **12.02** (the architecture-fad red flag: new model — KAN — same holes: no costs, no benchmark, implausible metric). The field's own people say so: [12] (no consistent baseline-beating standard), [18] (DRL profits are "false positives due to overfitting"), [20] (FinRL-Meta names low SNR, survivorship bias, backtest overfitting as the defining problems), [25] (2024 RL survey: still unresolved), [27] (offline RL policy "is not trustworthy" — memorizes one path). **And the two strongest corroborations: [42] — a rigorously-built SAC portfolio with real costs, 16 OOS folds over 2003–2026, three markets, HAC-robust inference — finds "no strategy achieves statistically significant excess returns relative to Buy & Hold"; and [77] — the FinRL Contests (200+ teams, 3 years), where the best CRYPTO RL agent essentially TIED holding Bitcoin (0.66% vs 0.74%) and a Sharpe-9.56 top team went NEGATIVE the very next out-of-sample week. Even a competing global crowd cannot beat buy-and-hold on crypto. That is our validated thesis, reproduced by RL proponents.**

**The architecture treadmill is a trap.** Every wave brings a new backbone — RNN/LSTM/GRU → CNN/DeepLOB → Transformers [6][7][8][22][40][85] → Mamba/state-space [56][74] → KAN [75] — pitched as the thing that finally cracks markets, and each arrives with the SAME methodological holes (no costs, no passive benchmark, a single window, an implausible headline). The honest comparative evidence keeps saying the opposite: linear baselines are hard to beat [8][67], GRU≈LSTM [55], a generic TCN beats LSTM [90], and the gains (when real) are tiny and cross-sectional [24][32]. **A striking, repeated pattern from the round-3 forecasting papers: the field's own strongest recent forecasters are SIMPLE MLP-or-decomposition designs, not heavy transformers** — N-HiTS [86], TSMixer [67], FreTS [92], TimeMixer [98], DLinear [8] all beat or match the elaborate transformers (Informer [6]/Autoformer [7]/FEDformer [85]) while being far cheaper; even the transformer "comebacks" win by inverting to cross-variate attention (iTransformer [88]) or via period-folding (TimesNet [87]) — i.e., their advantage is CROSS-VARIATE/periodic structure a single coin lacks. The practical conclusion for us is liberating: our cost-net, buy-and-hold-benchmarked, bootstrap gate is **architecture-agnostic** — it filters out the fad of the year automatically, so we never need to chase the new backbone.

**Where DL IS worth our time: generative models for SYNTHETIC TEST DATA, not for alpha.** [9] QuantGAN, [16] diffusion, [19] TimeGAN, [38] time-causal VAE, [26] neural SDEs, [31] agent-based MARL sim can produce synthetic price paths that reproduce stylized facts (fat tails, vol clustering) and could enrich our moving-block bootstrap with novel-but-plausible regimes. This is the single actionable DL lane for us — BUT [41] warns stylized-fact fidelity is fragile and architecture-dependent, so validate hard. Batch-3 strengthened the NON-adversarial generator case ([60] Neural-SDE with signature-kernel scores, public code) and the agent-based route ([66] RL agents → emergent flash crashes; [71] learned-summary-statistic calibration so a simulator actually MATCHES our coin). (Details below.)

**The thin-seam verdict (batch 3): nothing new is deployable as alpha for one coin.** (a) *Classical LSTM/GRU crypto* [55][73]: GRU≈LSTM, and the studies are prediction-error-only or cost-blind — accuracy ≠ alpha. (b) *Mamba/state-space* [56][74]: beats LSTM on price-level RMSE, but RMSE on a near-random-walk level is the easy metric, and the trading sims omit buy-and-hold / explicit costs. (c) *Deep RL for pairs/stat-arb* [57]: structurally a two-leg market-neutral strategy — our long-only single-coin advisor can't take the short leg; one-month tests, no buy-and-hold. (d) *Contrastive/self-supervised representation learning* [58][59][61][68][70][72]: powerful but CROSS-SECTIONAL and finance-unproven for cost-net trading; a single coin has no cross-asset contrast and weak/unstable seasonality. (e) *Adversarial robustness* [65][93] (+[35]): a learned trading policy can be degraded catastrophically (−214% reward) by an adversary merely trading against it [65], and a multivariate FORECASTER can be corrupted by sparse imperceptible perturbations to a correlated auxiliary series [93] — and crypto is more manipulable than equities, so opaque learned policies AND rich multivariate/alt-data inputs are a liability; simple transparent rules on few hard-to-game features are robust by construction. (f) *Price-as-image CNNs* [89][94]: a crypto-specific study [89] found the hyped GAF encoding far WORSE than a plain candlestick render (0.31 vs 0.89 AUC) and, like all this work, reports discrimination only — explicitly NOT cost-net return.

**Second actionable lane: borrow the FALSIFICATION / leakage-audit discipline.** [39] (run the pipeline on structural-null data and confirm it crowns nothing), [37] (8-type leakage taxonomy + model-info-sheet checklist), [34] (validate each input independently, not by end-to-end accuracy), [100] (select features by genuine causal signal, not a kitchen sink). These are concrete tests/checklists we can add, not models.

**The single sharpest lesson — significance, not point estimates (now with full-text numbers).** [99] (LSTM-ARIMA hybrid, Warsaw group) is a near-model of honest evaluation: cost-aware (0.1%), 23-year walk-forward, buy-and-hold benchmark — and the hybrid APPEARS to beat buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted Modified-IR basis on every index (long-only IR** 5.79 vs B&H 5.09 on S&P 500, 7.19 vs 0.66 FTSE, 3.04 vs 0.98 CAC) AND halves drawdown (S&P 500 28.95% vs 56.78%), **yet a paired t-test on the return stream returns p = 0.2420–0.9249 — it cannot reject "no real edge."** The only strategy that cleared significance was plain ARIMA long-only (p=0.0086–0.0362), which itself lost to B&H on the ratio. An attractive backtest that fails a significance test is not a crownable edge. Two sharpening details from the full read: (a) the headline lift lives in the LONG-SHORT variant (IR** 16.65/14.29, ensemble 70.54) that a long-only single-coin advisor cannot run, so the *long-only* numbers — the ones relevant to us — are the weakest; (b) IR** divides by max-drawdown, so a low-return/low-drawdown strategy can OUTRANK buy-and-hold on the ratio while being economically insignificant — a "drawdown-flattered ratio win" our verdict must not crown. This is exactly what our bootstrap weakest-link verdict + a Deflated-Sharpe/multiple-testing layer are for, and it directly motivates reporting significance AND statistical POWER on any excess return — never a bare point estimate. The power point is now quantified by [69]: a rigorous 34-fold equity walk-forward had only **~12% statistical power at the observed effect (d=0.17) and would have needed ~540 folds for 80%** — i.e. a handful of OOS windows can never adjudicate a thin edge, which is precisely why our 1000-path bootstrap manufactures the many resamples needed to have power. And [39] gives the multiplicity teeth: under a pure-noise null, **at K=100 trials the nominal 1.96 t-threshold is wrong 99.9% of the time**, and a bake-off of many CORRELATED variants does not escape this (the right count is the spectral K_eff, and any thresholding rule re-inflates it ~2.68×).

## Key themes

1. **Two paradigms.** (a) *Predict-then-trade* — forecast price/return/direction with LSTM/CNN/Transformer, then act ([3][4][5][6][7][8][11][17][21][22][24][33][40][51][52]). (b) *Learn-the-decision directly* — RL or deep hedging optimize a trading/hedging policy end-to-end against a P&L or risk objective ([1][2][10][13][15][18][23][27][28][29][36][42][43][45][48][49]). Paradigm (b) is more principled (no forecast→action gap) and has the best transferable ideas, but is data-hungry and overfits one regime.
2. **Generative models are a distinct, more promising lane FOR US** — [9][14][16][19][26][31][38][41][43][44][46][53][54] generate synthetic market data; relevant to test-data discipline, not alpha. Batch-2 deepened this: non-adversarial generators (diffusion [16][53], time-causal VAE [38], signature-MMD [46]) are now preferred over GANs for stability, and [54] gives the full validation protocol incl. leakage tests.
3. **The skeptical/meta/reproducibility literature is strong, consistent, and largely from insiders** — [8][12][18][20][24][25][27][34][37][39][47][50] all converge on: trivial baselines are hard to beat, costs/leakage/survivorship/seeds are routinely mishandled, and there is no reproducible standard. Batch-2 added the heavy hitters: [47] (RL results flip on the random seed alone), [37]+[39] (leakage manufactures fake alpha; falsify against structural nulls), [34] (don't validate a feature by end-to-end accuracy). Later rounds added: [77] (the FinRL Contests — 200+ teams — where crypto RL ties buy-and-hold and a Sharpe-9.56 top team goes negative the next week), [84] (Replicating Anomalies — 64% of 447 published anomalies vanish under conservative re-testing; "widespread p-hacking"), [62] (survey: ~1 of 94 DL stock papers took costs seriously), [69] (rigorous walk-forward equities: insignificant, p=0.34, with an honest 12% statistical-power admission), [63] (RL beats buy-and-hold only when the test regime was already in training), [99] (a cost-aware, 23-year walk-forward LSTM-ARIMA hybrid APPEARS to beat buy-and-hold but FAILS the paired-t significance test — appearance ≠ significant edge), and [100] (causal-feature crypto RL whose edge "varies across cryptocurrencies" — per-coin single-window wins are not robust).
4. **Costs and out-of-sample length are where headline results die** — papers that model costs honestly and use long out-of-sample windows ([15][24][42][52][69]) report small-or-zero edges; papers with astronomical returns ([21][23][10][28][56][75]) use short windows, no buy-and-hold compare, and/or undercount costs. **The decisive evidence: [42] (SAC, 16 folds, HAC-robust), [52] (hourly BTC, 27-fold walk-forward, bootstrap-adjusted), and [77] (FinRL Contests crypto: 0.66% vs 0.74% holding BTC) ALL find no robust outperformance vs buy-and-hold — our exact thesis, reproduced rigorously, twice on crypto specifically.**
5. **Costs push the optimal policy toward INACTION** — independently across deep hedging ([15][36]) and crypto ML ([52]): with realistic costs the optimal action is to trade less / hold, and a cost-proportional trade filter is the difference between viable and ruinous. This is the structural reason buy-and-hold is so hard to beat.
6. **The architecture treadmill.** Each wave — LSTM/GRU [55][73] → CNN/LOB [3][40] → Transformers [6][7][8][22][40][85] → Mamba/state-space [56][74] → KAN [75] — is pitched as the breakthrough and arrives with the SAME holes (no costs, no benchmark, one window, implausible metric). Honest comparisons say the opposite: GRU≈LSTM [55], TCN≥LSTM [90], linear ≥ deep [8][67], gains are tiny/cross-sectional [24][32]. **The strongest recent forecasters are deliberately SIMPLE (MLP/decomposition), not transformer:** N-HiTS [86], TSMixer [67], FreTS [92], TimeMixer [98], DLinear [8] all beat/match Informer/Autoformer/FEDformer [6][7][85]; transformer "comebacks" win only via cross-variate attention (iTransformer [88]) or periodicity (TimesNet [87]) — structure a single coin lacks. Our gate is architecture-agnostic, so we never need to chase the new backbone.
7. **Self-supervised / contrastive representation learning is finance-UNPROVEN and mostly cross-sectional** [58][59][61][68][70][72][83]. The wins are on general (non-financial, often multivariate) benchmarks; the cross-sectional methods [58] need a universe of assets; a single coin has no cross-asset contrast and weak/unstable seasonality. The one useful heuristic ([61]): in low-label/data-poor regimes (our case) a generative masked-autoencoder pretext [83] beats contrastive — but even a better representation of a near-efficient price need not yield cost-net alpha.

## Methods / findings that HOLD UP (and which don't)

**Credible / replicated:**
- **Simplicity wins in forecasting** [8][67][86][92][98]: one-layer linear (DLinear/NLinear) beats Informer/Autoformer/FEDformer by **20–50%** across virtually all 360+ comparisons on 9 datasets (full text [8]); self-attention is permutation-invariant → loses temporal order, and [8]'s decisive shuffle experiment proves it — **randomly shuffling the input barely changes transformer error (≈0% on Exchange-Rate) but degrades DLinear 27.26%**, i.e. the transformers were NOT using the time-ordering they claim to model. On the FINANCIAL (Exchange-Rate) set the gap is largest (DLinear 0.081 vs FEDformer 0.148, ~45%; even a naive Repeat-last beats the transformers), with the authors blaming transformer overfitting of "sudden change noise" — exactly crypto's character. The pattern repeats across the other forecasters — N-HiTS [86] (~20% better AND 50× faster, **54% of N-BEATS params**, and explicitly UNIVARIATE), TSMixer [67] (ablation: cross-variate machinery ≈ time-mixing-only on univariate-ish benchmarks; the 22.3% multivariate gain appears ONLY on M5 where real cross-variate signal exists), FreTS [92] (beats transformers ~33% but linear by only ~4%), TimeMixer [98] (4.7× lighter than PatchTST; decomposition ablation is load-bearing). [22] (PatchTST) / [88] (iTransformer) only partially rehabilitate transformers — and [88]'s full text is the cleanest proof of the cross-variate caveat: huge margins on 321–862-variate data but a near-TIE with one-line DLinear on the 21-variate Weather set, with the authors conceding a plain FFN suffices at low variate count. A generic TCN [90] also beats LSTM/GRU [55]. Consistent, now-quantified meta-lesson: well-designed *simple/linear* models are a hard baseline that beats transformers by a lot and linear-rivals by a little; deep complexity pays ONLY with cross-variate breadth/periodicity a single coin lacks — and a model can post strong benchmark numbers without using temporal structure at all.
- **ML's real asset-pricing edge is cross-sectional, nonlinear, and tiny in R²** [24][32]: doubling a small Sharpe via momentum/liquidity/volatility interactions across thousands of stocks; autoencoder factor models [32] win OOS only on the cross-section. Rigorous — and irrelevant to a single coin (no cross-section).
- **Generative models reproduce stylized facts** [9][16][19][38][46][54]; diffusion [16][53] / VAE [38] / signature methods [46][60][78] are more stable than GANs (no mode collapse, no adversarial instability); TimeGAN [19] + [54] contribute the standard validation metrics (discriminative score, TSTR, MMD, AND leakage tests). BUT [41] proves GAN stylized-fact fidelity is fragile and architecture-dependent — validate per-fact, never assume. **Batch-3 enriched this lane:** the SIGNATURE family is now well-represented and arguably most apt for path data — signature-MMD [46], signature-kernel Neural SDE [60] (public code), conditional Sig-Wasserstein GAN [78]; TAIL-aware generators exist — Tail-GAN [81] trains a VaR/ES-elicitable objective to get extreme losses right, the exact property [54] says must be a gating metric; agent-based simulators can emit emergent shocks/flash crashes [66] and be calibrated to match our coin via learned summary statistics [71]; and structural constraints (factor structure [80], no-arbitrage [26]) are the principled way to keep a generator economically plausible in data-poor settings.
- **Direct risk/Sharpe-objective policy learning is sound** [2][15][36][45]: deep hedging minimizes a convex risk measure; Deep Momentum Networks optimize a Sharpe loss; [36] tracks mean+variance of cost via two Q-functions; [45] uses a Dirichlet policy for valid weights. All "learn the decision" done right. [15] honestly shows the edge dies at ~2–3 bps cost; [36] shows costs → trade less.
- **Probabilistic/quantile + interpretable forecasting** [5] TFT: the honest way to forecast (distribution + variable importance).
- **Overfitting/leakage is testable** [18][37][39][47]: PBO/CSCV [18] gives an overfitting probability (concrete recipe: split into N=5, combinatorial validation, logit-rank, mass-below-zero; reject at 10% — full text shows it rejected the SAC/PPO-WF agents at 21.3%/17.5% and the honest caveat that in its 58-day CRASH window EVERY agent lost, the accepted ones just lost less); [37] an 8-type leakage taxonomy + model-info-sheet; [39] falsification against five structural nulls + a Backtest Inflation Factor / ΔZ gap, with the alarming proof that **under a noise null a naive search yields a "significant winner" ~100% of the time (99.9% false positives at K=100, 1.96 threshold)** and that correlated bake-off variants do not escape (count K_eff = K²/‖Σ‖_F²; thresholding re-inflates ~2.68×); [47] shows single-seed RL results are noise — changing ONLY the seed gives statistically distinct curves (**t=−9.09, p=0.0016**) and architecture alone swings results 45× → demand many seeds + significance + a confidence band.
- **SAC > DDPG for stability** [28][42]: entropy regularization + twin critics make SAC/TD3 far more stable than vanilla DDPG in noisy/volatile markets — the consistent algorithm-choice lesson if continuous-action RL is ever used.

**Overstated / do NOT hold up:**
- **DRL/DL "beats the market" crypto returns** [10][21][23][28][56][73][75]: bull-market drift, survivorship/selection, leakage, short single-path windows, missing buy-and-hold benchmark. Not reproducible. Directly contradicted by rigorous [42][52][77] (no significant edge vs buy-and-hold; FinRL Contests crypto ties holding).
- **Directional / price-level / image accuracy → profit leap** [3][4][11][21][33][40][55][56][74][82][89]: high classification accuracy, low price-level RMSE, or high image-CNN AUC reported without cost-net P&L or tradability. Accuracy ≠ alpha — [40] proves it (F1 collapses once the threshold = the spread); [52] proves it (positive gross → −80% net at 10 bps); the crypto GAF study [89] is openly classification-only (best 0.89 AUC, "discrimination ability rather than risk-adjusted returns after costs"). Note the level-vs-return trap: low RMSE on a near-random-walk *price level* [55][56][74] is easy and meaningless for trading. And the input-transform trap: the hyped GAF encoding was DRAMATICALLY worse than a raw candlestick render [89] — a fancier input is not automatically better.
- **"Complex transformer = better time-series modeling"** as a general claim [6][7][85]: refuted by [8][67][86][92][98] (simple MLP/decomposition designs beat or match Informer/Autoformer/FEDformer). The newer "Mamba/state-space [56][74] / KAN [75] cracks markets" claims repeat the pattern — and [55] shows GRU≈LSTM, [90] TCN≥LSTM. (Honest caveat: pure-DL CAN beat statistical baselines on broad, heterogeneous forecasting [79][86] N-BEATS/N-HiTS — but by modest margins, breadth-dependent, never cost-net on one asset.)
- **Single-window Sharpe as evidence of robustness** [13][23][28][56][75]: a Sharpe of 1–12 on one window has enormous error bars — [47] shows even the random seed can produce it, and [77] shows a Sharpe-9.56 team going negative the next week. A Sharpe > ~4 on one asset [75] is a leakage/in-sample/zero-cost red flag, not a result.
- **Offline RL trained on one history** [27][63]: "your offline policy is not trustworthy" — it memorizes the single historical path; [63] shows RL only beats buy-and-hold when the test regime was already in the training window.
- **A black-box learned model is adversarially fragile** [65][93] (+[35]) — now with quantified attack potency: an adversary merely TRADING against a DRL agent (gray-box, no model access) cut its reward by **214%** [65]; a multivariate forecaster's error inflates **+215%** when an attacker makes sparse, imperceptible perturbations to half of the correlated AUXILIARY series (never touching the target), and the best defense recovers only part of it (wQL 1.03→0.65) [93]; a stock-sentiment model is flipped with just **1 injected tweet / 1 word (≈17% success, ≈25% portfolio loss over 2 years)** [35]. The common thread is a realistic threat model — the attacker just participates in the same market/feed — and the consistent finding that MORE inputs = MORE fragility ([93]: multivariate is 3× more attackable than univariate; the cross-series channel is a NEW attack surface). Crypto is far more manipulable than equities, so opaque learned policies AND rich multivariate/alt-data inputs are a liability; simple transparent rules on few hard-to-game features are robust by construction, and INPUT PARSIMONY is a robustness property, not just a simplicity preference.
- **An attractive risk-adjusted backtest is not a SIGNIFICANT edge** [99] (+[69]): a cost-aware, 23-year walk-forward LSTM-ARIMA hybrid looked like it beat buy-and-hold on Modified-IR, yet a paired t-test could not reject "no real edge"; [69] reports an honest 12% statistical power. Demand a significance measure (and a Deflated-Sharpe/multiple-testing correction), not a bare point estimate — the whole reason for our many-resample bootstrap.
- **Per-coin / single-window "beats buy-and-hold" is regime/asset-conditional luck** [100] (+[63]): the causal-feature crypto RL [100] reports an edge that "varies across cryptocurrencies" (and includes a stablecoin in the universe); [63] shows RL beats holding only when the test regime was already in training. A winner on some coins / one window is not a robust strategy — exactly what the weakest-link verdict across 1000 paths is designed to reject.
- **GNN relational-graph edge** [11][33]: may rest on graphs that aren't actually informative — [34] shows the literature validates graphs only by downstream accuracy, conflating graph quality with model quality.
- **Models fail exactly in crises** [51]: learned vol forecasters degrade most in high-vol regimes (MAPE >16% in 2008) — worst when it matters most.
- **Self-supervised representation learning as single-coin alpha** [58][59][61][68][70][72][83]: powerful and fast-moving, but finance-unproven for cost-net trading, and the wins are cross-sectional / multivariate / on general benchmarks — a single coin has no cross-asset contrast.

## Actionable takeaways for our advisor

1. **Keep the always-benchmark-vs-buy-and-hold, cost-net, bootstrap gate. It is precisely what this literature lacks — and the rigorous papers that DO use it reach our exact conclusion.** [8][10][12][18][20][21][23] show that without a trivial baseline + cost discipline + multi-path validation, DL/RL "wins" are illusions; and the two most rigorous, cost-aware, bootstrap/HAC-tested studies [42][52] independently find NO significant edge over buy-and-hold ([52] on hourly BTC). Our gate is a competitive advantage and is externally vindicated.
2. **Highest-value DL experiment for us = a generative simulator for TEST DATA — now with a clear recipe and a strong shortlist.** Prototype a NON-adversarial generator — diffusion [16][53], time-causal VAE [38], or a SIGNATURE-based model (signature-MMD [46], signature-kernel Neural SDE [60] which has public code, or conditional Sig-WGAN [78]) — all preferred over plain GANs [9][41] for stability; the signature family is arguably most apt for path data. Fit to our coin to augment the moving-block bootstrap with novel stylized-fact-preserving regimes, with a **conditional STRESS/crash knob** [43] and ideally a **tail-aware objective** (Tail-GAN's VaR/ES-elicitability [81]) so the synthetic paths actually contain worse-than-observed crashes the bootstrap can't produce. Validate with the full [54] suite: distributional+temporal fidelity (MMD/KS, vol-clustering autocorr), TAIL-event fidelity (VAEs over-smooth tails — a dealbreaker [54][81]), downstream utility (TSTR [19]), and LEAKAGE (nearest-neighbor + membership-inference [54]) to enforce the no-test-path-leak guardrail. (Agent-based simulators [66][71] are a heavier alternative that can emit emergent shocks; not the first prototype.)
3. **NEW high-value, low-cost test: falsify our own pipeline against structural nulls** [39]. Run the entire bake-off + ranking on each of the FIVE synthetic nulls [39] names — white noise, regime-switching volatility, bid-ask bounce, zero-alpha factor, GARCH(1,1) — and assert it crowns NOTHING above buy-and-hold. This is high-value because [39] PROVES that under a noise null a naive search produces a "winner" with near-certainty (**99.9% false-positive rate at K=100 trials with the 1.96 t-threshold; 100% for orthogonal feature-mining at K=400**), so a pipeline that does NOT crown noise is positive evidence it is leak-free, and one that does has a demonstrable bug. Report the in-sample-vs-walk-forward magnitude gap ΔZ and a Backtest Inflation Factor as the diagnostic. Pair with a leakage-audit checklist (a "model info sheet" [37]) for any learned component. This is a test/CI item, not a model.
4. **If we ever add a learned signal, the rules are fixed by this literature:** (a) benchmark against a DLinear-style linear baseline AND buy-and-hold BEFORE anything else [8][22] — and prefer a simple MLP/decomposition forecaster [67][86][92][98] or a causal TCN [90] over a transformer if a model is needed at all; (b) forecast a DISTRIBUTION, not a point [5]; (c) if learning a policy, optimize a risk/Sharpe objective directly, add turnover regularization, use a Dirichlet parametrization for valid sizing [2][15][36][45]; (d) select inputs by genuine (causal) signal and validate each independently — not a kitchen sink [34][100] — and keep them few and hard-to-game (rich multivariate/alt-data inputs are an adversarial liability [93][35]); (e) report a cost-sensitivity curve and expect the edge to vanish by a few bps [15][52]; (f) report across many SEEDS + bootstrap paths with a SIGNIFICANCE measure and statistical power [42][47][69][99] — an attractive risk-adjusted backtest that fails a significance test is not an edge [99]. Do not reach for a Transformer.
5. **Borrow the overfitting/leakage diagnostics, not the models — and add a multiple-testing correction.** Consider a PBO/CSCV [18] overfitting probability and/or a Backtest-Inflation-Factor / ΔZ gap [39] as complementary readouts beside our weakest-link verdict. **Strongly consider a selection-bias correction** (Deflated-Sharpe / multiple-testing adjustment) on the bake-off: [84] (64% of 447 anomalies vanish under conservative re-testing; "widespread p-hacking") and [39] both warn that trying MANY strategies guarantees a lucky in-sample winner — a large bake-off is exactly that multiple-testing machine — and [39] adds the subtle, decisive twist that a bake-off of CORRELATED strategy variants does not escape multiplicity: the right count is the spectral K_eff = K²/‖Σ‖_F², and any thresholding/decision rule re-inflates it (~2.68×), so naively counting "we only have a few distinct strategy families" understates the inflation. FinRL-Crypto's PBO-rejection at 10% [18][77] is a concrete precedent. Adopt the survivorship-bias warning [20] if we expand beyond one coin. **Report STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE AND POWER honestly** [69][99]: [99] shows a strategy can beat buy-and-hold on every risk-adjusted ratio yet fail a paired t-test (p=0.24–0.92), and [69] shows a 34-fold walk-forward had only ~12% power (needing ~540 folds for 80%) — with few independent regime-folds you literally cannot distinguish a tiny edge from zero, which is the core argument for the bootstrap's many resamples (manufacture the power) and for never crowning a bare point estimate or a drawdown-flattered ratio.
6. **Treat all DRL/EIIE/DQN/SAC/DDPG trading machinery as multi-asset-only and unproven** [1][10][13][23][28][42][45]. Irrelevant to a single coin; not worth the overfitting surface. Reusable *sub-ideas* only: validation-Sharpe agent-selection + turbulence risk-off [13], turnover regularization [15], two-Q mean-variance cost objective [36], Dirichlet sizing [45], SAC-over-DDPG if forced to choose [28].
7. **Test a cost-proportional trade filter as a strategy primitive** [52]: only act when the expected move exceeds a multiple of round-trip cost. This is the single mechanism that turned ruinous crypto ML strategies viable — a concrete, cheap thing to add to the bake-off (and likely STILL won't beat buy-and-hold, which is the honest expected result).

## Open questions / things worth testing in our app

- **Synthetic-data prototype (TOP candidate):** Can a diffusion [16][53] / time-causal VAE [38] / signature-based ([46][60][78]) generator fit to our coin produce bootstrap-complementary paths that the gate treats as a *harder* test (more stress, novel regimes)? Add a conditional stress/crash intensity [43] and a tail-aware objective [81]. Validate with the full [54] suite incl. tail-fidelity + leakage. Start with [60] (signature-kernel Neural SDE, public code) to minimize prototyping cost.
- **Null-data falsification (cheap, high value):** Does our bake-off+ranking pipeline correctly crown NOTHING on white-noise / zero-edge-GARCH / bid-ask-bounce nulls [39]? If not, find the leak. A direct, implementable CI test.
- **Multiple-testing correction on the bake-off (high value):** Does adding a Deflated-Sharpe / selection-bias adjustment [84][39] (or PBO-rejection [18][77]) change which strategy the bake-off crowns? Hypothesis: it shrinks or eliminates apparent winners, matching the "64% of anomalies vanish" result [84]. This is the single most literature-supported gate upgrade.
- **Cost-proportional trade filter:** Does a "trade only if expected move > k·cost" gate [52] change any verdict in our bake-off? Hypothesis: it reduces turnover but still doesn't beat buy-and-hold.
- **Linear-baseline sanity check:** Does a DLinear-style trend/seasonal+linear forecaster [8] beat buy-and-hold net of costs under the frozen gate? Hypothesis: no — but it's the cheap minimal "is there ANY time-series signal" probe.
- **Risk-measure sizing:** Could a deep-hedging-style convex-risk objective [2][36] or Sharpe-loss-with-turnover-penalty [15] + Dirichlet output [45] inform a *position-sizing* policy (how much of the €200, when to de-risk) rather than a directional signal? Expect the cost curve to bite.
- **Overfitting metric:** Is PBO/CSCV [18] or a Backtest-Inflation-Factor [39] worth wiring in as a second opinion alongside the bootstrap weakest-link verdict?
