# Papers — Data (the DATA itself)

Ledger for the **data** topic: the data pipeline, distinct from `backtesting`
(which owns evaluation methodology). Scope: test/train/validation splits & data
leakage; overfitting from the DATA angle; Monte-Carlo & synthetic data generation
(bootstrap, block-bootstrap, GANs/diffusion for market data, agent-based market
simulators); point-in-time / as-of data & survivorship; data labeling (triple-
barrier, horizons); data quality/cleaning; alternative data; train/test
contamination; sample size & data requirements; cross-validation for time series
(purging/embargo from the data side); data augmentation.

> **Our app:** Rust single-coin crypto investment **advisor** (paper/sim only).
> Pick coin + budget → bake off strategies on a window → rank under a FROZEN
> robustness gate (1000-path **moving-block bootstrap**; weakest-link verdict;
> buy-and-hold always the benchmark) → forward paper-trade. Thesis: no active
> strategy robustly beats holding, net of costs.

### [1] tsbootstrap: Enhancing Time Series Analysis with Advanced Bootstrapping Techniques
- **Authors / Venue:** Sankalp Gilda, Benedikt Heidrich, Franz Kiraly / arXiv (q-fin/stat)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2404.15227
- **% read:** 60%
- **Summary:** Introduces a Python package unifying the major dependent-data bootstrap families: Moving Block (MBB), Circular Block (CBB), Stationary (random block length, SBB), Non-Overlapping (NBB), Tapered (TBB, window functions to reduce edge effects), plus model-based methods (residual, sieve/AR, Markov, distribution, statistic-preserving). Core idea: i.i.d. bootstrap is invalid for autocorrelated series; resampling *blocks* preserves within-block dependence and asymptotically imitates the full dependence structure as block length → ∞ at the right rate. Notes block-length selection is the key tuning knob (cites Bühlmann & Künsch 1999) and flags adaptive autocorrelation-driven block sizing as future work.
- **Relevance to our system:** Directly maps the design space our FROZEN robustness gate sits in. We use **moving-block** specifically; this catalogs the alternatives (tapered = fewer edge artifacts; stationary = random block length avoids a single brittle length; circular = no end-of-series truncation bias). Worth a sensitivity check: does our verdict change under tapered/stationary vs. fixed-length MBB? If the weakest-link verdict is stable across bootstrap variants, that strengthens "no active strategy beats holding"; if it flips, our block scheme is doing load-bearing work and needs documenting.

### [2] When Alpha Disappears: A One-Switch Benchmark for Decision-Time Leakage in Financial Backtests
- **Authors / Venue:** Fan Zhang, Zhen Li, Sijia Peng, Yu Chen (U. Tokyo, HKUST-GZ) / arXiv (q-fin.RM)
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2605.23959
- **% read:** 75%
- **Summary:** Chronological train/test splits are necessary but NOT sufficient for out-of-sample validity — two backtests with identical splits can access different information at decision time. Uses a "one-switch paired comparison": from a decision-time-consistent reference (features use only day-t close, trades execute t+1 open, labels open-to-open), each test flips exactly ONE pipeline component. Five violations: TEMP_CENTER (centered rolling windows that peek at future), NORM_GLOBAL (normalize on full sample not just train), STRUCT_GRAPH (correlation graph using future returns), EXEC_CLOSE (same-day-close fills), EXEC_OPEN (using the full day-t OHLCV bar while assuming an open fill). Result: TEMP_CENTER and EXEC_OPEN dominate, inflating Sharpe by 15–30+ points consistently across LightGBM/Graph/LSTM/Transformer and across US/China and 2016–2024; the other three are near-negligible. Tree/graph models most vulnerable.
- **Relevance to our system:** This is the canonical "leakage isn't just about the split" warning, with a quantified ranking of *which* leaks matter. Two are directly actionable for us: (1) **centered/look-ahead rolling features** — any indicator (SMA/EMA/Bollinger/RSI) must use only trailing data through the decision bar; a centered window is a silent Sharpe-inflator. (2) **execution alignment** — if a signal is computed from a bar's close, the fill must be the *next* bar, never that same bar's open/high/low. Our forward paper-trade and bake-off must both honor t+1 (or next-bar) execution. The "global normalization" warning also applies if we ever standardize features for an ML overlay. Suggests an explicit leakage-audit checklist as a gate alongside robustness.

### [3] Purged Cross-Validation (purgedcv): leakage-aware CV for dependent data
- **Authors / Venue:** Evgenii Lazarev / Journal of Open Source Software (JOSS)
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** https://github.com/eslazarev/purged-cross-validation (paper/paper.md); implements López de Prado's purging/embargo/CPCV
- **% read:** 60%
- **Summary:** Time-series labels overlap with neighboring observations, breaking k-fold's independence assumption and causing forward leakage (test rows in train) and backward leakage (serial correlation drags test structure into train), inflating apparent skill. Implements purging (drop train labels overlapping the test window), embargo (drop a buffer after each test fold for reaction-lag), walk-forward, group-purged k-fold, and Combinatorial Purged CV (CPCV) with multi-path backtest reconstruction; also ships Probabilistic/Deflated Sharpe and Minimum Track Record Length. **Controlled experiments quantify the leak:** on pure-noise synthetic data naive k-fold gets R² 0.83–0.91 (spurious) while purged k-fold collapses below baseline; on **Bitcoin returns naive R² ≈ +0.85 vs purged R² ≈ −1.2**; earthquakes +0.65 → −1.24; adding one monotone feature inflates naive R² to 0.99 (purged −1.52). Notes leakage *type/magnitude is deployment-dependent* (a household-energy case showed a tiny in-distribution gap but a 6% drop on unseen customers).
- **Relevance to our system:** The Bitcoin number is a direct, sobering data point for *our* asset class: naive CV manufactures ~0.85 R² of pure illusion on crypto returns. Confirms our instinct to never trust a single in-sample fit and to lean on out-of-sample + bootstrap robustness. If we ever add an ML/label-based overlay (e.g. triple-barrier targets), purging+embargo are mandatory, not optional. CPCV is the more rigorous cousin of our single moving-block path — it generates *many* backtest paths from combinatorial train/test splits, conceptually aligned with our 1000-path bootstrap but operating on the split structure rather than resampling returns. Deflated/Probabilistic Sharpe (and MinTRL) are concrete metrics we could surface next to the buy-and-hold benchmark to express "how much of this edge survives multiple-testing skepticism."

### [4] Can GANs Learn the Stylized Facts of Financial Time Series?
- **Authors / Venue:** Sohyeon Kwon (NCSOFT), Yongjae Lee (UNIST) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2410.09850
- **% read:** 60%
- **Summary:** Critical evaluation of whether GANs actually capture financial time-series dynamics, using *known* stochastic processes as ground truth (Brownian/Geometric Brownian motion, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck mean reversion, Jump-Diffusion, Heston stochastic vol) so the "true" properties are known. Tested five generators (MLP, MLP-CNN, LSTM, GRU, TCN) with MLP-CNN discriminators. Findings: GANs handle simple GBM/log-return distributions well, but **fail on the hard, finance-defining features** — mean-reversion speed is poorly learned (TCN especially), jump events show the worst distribution match, Heston volatility comes out too platykurtic (under-dispersed tails), and **multivariate generation collapses to depending on a single variable**. Conclusion: naive GAN application does NOT reliably reproduce the intricate properties of financial series; architecture choice and validation are essential.
- **Relevance to our system:** A crucial reality check before we ever use GAN-synthesized data as a test set for our advisor. The features GANs fail at — fat tails, vol clustering/jumps, cross-asset dependence — are *exactly* the features that determine whether a strategy survives a crisis. A backtest on GAN data that misses jumps/heavy tails would be dangerously optimistic. Strong argument that our **moving-block bootstrap of REAL returns** is safer than GAN synthesis: the bootstrap can't invent dynamics the asset never had, it only reshuffles real blocks (preserving real tails/clustering within a block). If we ever explore synthetic stress data, prefer methods with explicit tail/jump control over a black-box GAN, and validate against stylized-fact checklists.

### [5] Quant GANs: Deep Generation of Financial Time Series
- **Authors / Venue:** Magnus Wiese, Robert Knobloch, Ralf Korn, Peter Kretschmer / Quantitative Finance
- **Year:** 2019 (arXiv) / 2020 (journal)
- **Source:** arXiv:1907.06673
- **% read:** 50%
- **Summary:** The seminal GAN-for-market-data paper. Generator and discriminator are both Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs), chosen to capture long-range dependence (volatility clusters). Trained on ~9 years of S&P 500; reproduces volatility clustering, leverage effect, and serial autocorrelation with high distributional fidelity at small and large lags. A distinctive feature: the generator is constructed so the induced process admits a transition to its risk-neutral distribution (useful for pricing). Authors acknowledge training was irregular / did not cleanly converge — a recurring GAN pain point.
- **Relevance to our system:** Establishes the upper bound of what synthetic market-data generation can do (and what it costs in training fragility). For our advisor, the takeaway is comparative: QuantGAN *can* reproduce vol clustering/leverage, but [4] shows naive GANs miss jumps/tails/cross-asset structure, and training is unstable. For a single-coin advisor that needs a *trustworthy, reproducible* test distribution, a deterministic moving-block bootstrap of real returns is far cheaper and has no convergence risk. Keep QuantGAN in mind only if we ever need *counterfactual* paths the historical window can't supply (e.g. unseen regimes) — but validate any such output against stylized-fact checks first.

### [6] Deep Calibration of Market Simulations using Neural Density Estimators and Embedding Networks
- **Authors / Venue:** Stillman, Baggott, Lyon, Zhang, Zhu, Chen, Vytelingum / ICAIF '23 (4th ACM Intl. Conf. on AI in Finance)
- **Year:** 2023
- **Source:** arXiv:2311.11913
- **% read:** 50%
- **Summary:** Tackles the "simulation-reality gap" in agent-based market simulators (ABMs): they're theoretically sound but hard to calibrate to real data, and traditional calibration cherry-picks hand-chosen stylized facts (subjective, unscalable). Uses simulation-based inference: neural density estimators (Neural Spline Flows, Masked Autoregressive Flows) to approximate the posterior over ABM parameters without a tractable likelihood, plus embedding networks (MLPs) that learn low-dimensional summaries of simulation output directly — removing the dependence on hand-picked stylized facts. For our single-coin advisor we don't need a full LOB simulator — we trade on bars, not order flow — so ABMs are out of scope as a generator. BUT the *methodological* lesson is useful: "hand-picked stylized facts introduce subjective bias" is a caution for how we'd validate ANY synthetic test data. If we ever score synthetic vs real data, a learned divergence (embedding-net style) is less gameable than eyeballing a few ACF plots.

### [7] Survivorship Bias in Emerging Market Small-Cap Indices: Evidence from India's NIFTY Smallcap 250
- **Authors / Venue:** Harjot Singh Ranse / arXiv
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2603.19380
- **% read:** 50%
- **Summary:** Reconstructs the true historical index composition (1,437 stocks, 2016–2025) using bhavcopy data that *includes delisted securities*, then compares an equal-weight portfolio of *current* constituents vs *all historical members*. Survivorship bias is large in this emerging small-cap universe: **annual returns overstated by 4.94 pp (23.3% inflation), Sharpe inflated by 0.097 (9.1%)**. Index turnover was 82.5%, split across delisted (16.1%), graduated-to-larger-index (33.1%), and demoted-within-smallcap (33.2%) — all three contribute to bias. Concludes survivorship bias materially exceeds developed-market levels.
- **Relevance to our system:** Survivorship bias is fundamentally a *universe-selection* problem, and our advisor sidesteps the worst of it by being **single-coin** — we don't pick survivors from a cross-section, we analyze one chosen coin's actual history. BUT a subtler crypto analog bites us: (1) **coin selection** — if the operator only ever points the advisor at coins that *still exist and look good today* (BTC/ETH), the validated "holding wins" thesis is itself conditioned on survivors; thousands of dead altcoins would tell a darker story. (2) **delisting/de-pegging** — our data pipeline must decide how to treat a coin that goes to zero or gets delisted mid-window; silently truncating to the survived portion is a look-ahead/survivorship leak. Worth a note in product scope: results are conditional on the coin surviving the window.

### [8] Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism: The Effects of Backtest Overfitting on Out-of-Sample Performance
- **Authors / Venue:** David H. Bailey, Jonathan Borwein, Marcos López de Prado, Qiji Jim Zhu / Notices of the AMS, May 2014, pp. 458–471
- **Year:** 2014
- **Source:** Notices of the AMS 61(5):458–471 (open: davidhbailey.com / SDM-LBL PDF; SSRN 2308659)
- **% read:** 50%
- **Summary:** Foundational warning that backtesting is *trivially* overfit. Central quantified result: there is a direct relationship between the **number of strategy trials** and the **minimum backtest length** needed to avoid a spuriously high Sharpe — try enough variants and you are essentially *guaranteed* to find an in-sample Sharpe of (e.g.) 1+ on pure noise. Derives how many independent trials produce an inflated Sharpe by chance (the multiple-comparisons problem in finance). Argues that reporting a backtest *without disclosing the number of trials* is scientifically meaningless and "mathematically irresponsible," because reported performance is then systematically optimistic.
- **Relevance to our system:** This is the theoretical backbone of our whole product thesis. Our bake-off tries **many** strategies (SMA/EMA/MACD/RSI/Bollinger + composed families + tuned params) on one window — exactly the multiple-testing setup that *manufactures* a winner. The paper says: the more configs we sweep, the higher the in-sample Sharpe we'll find by luck, and the longer the window we'd need to trust it. Two concrete implications: (a) our robustness gate + buy-and-hold benchmark is the *right* defense — it forces the crowned config to beat a fixed null, not just the other tried configs; (b) we should **track and surface the trial count (N strategies × N param settings)** so the operator knows how much selection pressure produced the winner. Strong support for "no active strategy robustly beats holding": the few that look good in-sample are exactly what this paper predicts from overfitting.

### [9] Profitability of Simple Technical Trading Rules of Chinese Stock Exchange Indexes
- **Authors / Venue:** Hong Zhu, Zhi-Qiang Jiang, Sai-Ping Li, Wei-Xing Zhou / Physica A (arXiv preprint)
- **Year:** 2015
- **Source:** arXiv:1504.04254
- **% read:** 60%
- **Summary:** Tests 48 classic technical rules (24 variable-MA, 18 fixed-MA, 6 trading-range-break) on Chinese index data, applying **White's Reality Check** with the **stationary bootstrap** (Politis-Romano) across four smoothing/expected-block-length parameters (q = 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0) to correct for data snooping. This is strong third-party corroboration that "no simple technical rule robustly beats holding once you pay costs." Also a methodological note: they vary the bootstrap's expected block length (via q) — a precedent for the **block-length sensitivity check** I flagged for our gate [1]. The pattern "significant gross, dead net-of-costs" is exactly what our cost-aware ranking should keep catching; it's a reason to make sure our cost model is never accidentally zeroed (a no-op cost would resurrect phantom alpha).

### [10] Stock Price Prediction Using Triple Barrier Labeling and Raw OHLCV Data: Evidence from Korean Markets
- **Authors / Venue:** Sungwoo Kang, Jong-Kook Kim (Korea University) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2025
- **Source:** arXiv:2504.02249
- **% read:** 50%
- **Summary:** Uses López de Prado's triple-barrier labeling (take-profit, stop-loss, time-limit barriers) to turn raw OHLCV into a 3-class target, then compares 4 deep models (LSTM, TCN, ResNet, CNN) and 11 tree/ML models. Optimal labeling: 29-day horizon with ±9% barriers, giving a balanced label split (~36% time-limit, ~35% take-profit, ~29% stop-loss). Key results: LSTM on *raw* OHLCV (F1 0.4312) essentially ties XGBoost on engineered indicators (F1 0.4316); full OHLCV beats close-only and close+volume; best LSTM is tiny (hidden size 8, 100-day window). Frames triple-barrier as path-aware labeling that fixes fixed-horizon's blindness to the trajectory between entry and exit.
- **Relevance to our system:** Two takeaways. (1) **Labeling discipline** — triple-barrier is the right way to define "did this trade work?" because it respects path (a position that hit a -9% stop is NOT the same as one that drifted to +1% over the horizon, even if endpoints match). If we ever score signals or build an ML overlay, triple-barrier labels (volatility-scaled barriers, explicit time limit) beat naive next-bar/fixed-horizon returns. (2) **Sobering effect sizes** — even with good labeling and deep models, F1 ≈ 0.43 on a 3-class problem is barely better than chance; raw OHLCV ties hand-engineered features. This reinforces that predictive edge in price data is thin, consistent with our "holding wins" thesis and a caution against over-investing in feature engineering or model complexity.

### [12] Data Augmentation for Time-Series Classification: A Comprehensive Survey
- **Authors / Venue:** Zijun Gao, Haibao Liu, Lingbo Li (Warwick / QMUL / Nanjing) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2023
- **Source:** arXiv:2310.10060
- **% read:** 50%
- **Summary:** Surveys time-series augmentation across five families: transformation-based (jittering, scaling, rotation, permutation, time/magnitude/frequency warping), pattern-based (mine & recombine sub-patterns), generative (GAN/VAE/autoencoder), decomposition-based (EMD/STL → augment components → reassemble), and automated (RL/search for augmentation policies). Empirically, Random Permutation and guided warping (RGWs) most consistently beat the no-augmentation baseline (ResNet 85.7% vs 85.0%), but gains are *small and dataset-dependent*. **Strong caution:** "benefits of augmentation are not universally applicable"; aggressive transforms (SPAWNER, Rotation) introduce artifacts that deviate too far from the real data and can corrupt learned representations; pattern-based methods can break the temporal continuity LSTMs rely on.
- **Relevance to our system:** Directly relevant to the "should we augment our limited price history?" question. The survey's headline caution is the key takeaway for finance: many augmentations (rotation, jitter, magnitude warp) are *physically meaningless* for price series and inject artifacts — e.g. rotating a return series destroys the sign/leverage structure; magnitude-warping fabricates volatility the asset never had. The augmentations that are *defensible* for us are exactly the dependence-preserving ones: **block bootstrap / permutation of returns** (which our gate already does). So this validates our approach and warns against importing generic CV-style augmentation. If we ever augment, prefer block-resampling over warping/jitter, and *always* re-check stylized facts after.

### [13] Asset Returns and Volatility Clustering in Financial Time Series
- **Authors / Venue:** Jie-Jun Tseng, Sai-Ping Li / Physica A (arXiv preprint)
- **Year:** 2010
- **Source:** arXiv:1002.0284
- **% read:** 60%
- **Summary:** Empirical study of the canonical stylized facts of returns: heavy tails, volatility clustering, slow-decaying autocorrelation of *absolute* returns (vs near-zero autocorrelation of raw returns), and gain/loss asymmetry. Clever ablation: they replace empirical returns with Gaussian values while *preserving the temporal positions* of large fluctuations — and the slow nonlinear-ACF decay survives. Conclusion: **heavy tails are NOT what causes the slow autocorrelation decay; the clustering (temporal positions) of large fluctuations is.** Introduce a clustering index R_n (bounded 1 to √n) and find big losses cluster more severely than big gains across NASDAQ, S&P 500, HSI, MSFT, FX, and WTI.
- **Relevance to our system:** Defines the properties any honest test data MUST preserve, and clarifies *why* our resampling choice matters. The key insight — that the **temporal positioning** of large moves (not just their fat-tailed magnitude) drives the dependence structure — is precisely the thing an i.i.d. bootstrap destroys and a **block bootstrap preserves** (large moves stay glued to their neighbors inside a block). That's the theoretical justification for our moving-block gate over plain resampling. The loss>gain clustering asymmetry is also a reason our block length must be long enough to contain a typical volatility cluster; too-short blocks would shred crash dynamics and understate drawdown risk — a concrete input to choosing/justifying our block length.

### [14] The GT-Score: A Robust Objective Function for Reducing Overfitting in Data-Driven Trading Strategies
- **Authors / Venue:** Alexander Pearson Sheppert / arXiv
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2602.00080
- **% read:** 60%
- **Summary:** Proposes a composite optimization objective designed to resist overfitting: GT_Score = (μ · ln(z) · r²) / σ_d, where μ = mean return, z = Z-score of outperformance vs benchmark, r² = return consistency, σ_d = downside deviation. The ln(z) term is a "significance gate" that rejects strategies lacking genuine outperformance vs the benchmark; r² and σ_d penalize erratic/downside-heavy equity. Validated with walk-forward (nine sequential 4yr-train/2yr-validate splits on 50 S&P 500 stocks, 2010–2024) plus Monte-Carlo over 15 seeds (9,000 trials) against Simple-profit, Sharpe, and Sortino objectives. Result: ~98% better generalization ratio (0.365 vs 0.185), validation returns retain ~2x more of training performance, p ≪ 0.01 — at the cost of slightly lower raw test returns.
- **Relevance to our system:** Conceptually parallel to our ranking philosophy, and a candidate refinement. Today we rank under a robustness gate with buy-and-hold as benchmark; GT-Score *bakes the benchmark-significance test directly into the objective* (the ln(z) gate) rather than applying it only as a downstream filter. The lesson: **optimize for generalization, not in-sample return** — choosing the objective that survives walk-forward beats choosing the highest backtest Sharpe. The downside-deviation and consistency terms also echo our weakest-link verdict (penalize fragility, not just reward mean). Worth testing whether ranking our bake-off configs by a GT-Score-like composite changes which config gets crowned vs raw return/Sharpe — likely fewer false winners. Also reinforces walk-forward + Monte-Carlo (their 9,000 trials) as the right validation harness.

### [15] AI-Driven Alpha Decay: Algorithmic Homogenization, Reflexive Signal Erosion, and the Paradox of Intelligent Markets
- **Authors / Venue:** Shuchen Meng, Xupeng Chen (NYU) / arXiv (q-fin.GN)
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2605.23905
- **% read:** 50%
- **Summary:** Argues widespread AI/ML adoption in asset management is self-defeating via three mechanisms: (1) **signal crowding** — many systems trained on identical data with similar architectures converge, arbitraging mispricings and cutting signal half-life from 5–7 years (pre-AI) to ~18 months at current adoption; (2) **performative/reflexive erosion** — models retrain on market data already contaminated by the price impact of their own prior trades, attenuating the very coefficient they exploit; (3) **Red Queen dynamics** — firms escalate AI spend to hold position even as aggregate net alpha → 0. Derives alpha half-life h(φ)=ln2/[θ+δ(φ)], convex-decreasing in adoption, with a "signal-extinction cascade" beyond a critical adoption threshold. Notes idiosyncratic human/differentiated strategies retain longer alpha than homogeneous AI.
- **Relevance to our system:** Strong macro-level support for our "no active strategy robustly beats holding" thesis, *and* a caution about the shelf life of any edge we might find. Even if our bake-off ever crowns a genuinely-OOS-robust active strategy, this paper says crowded/commoditized signals (and SMA/RSI/MACD are maximally commoditized) have collapsing half-lives — by the time a retail advisor acts on them, the alpha is gone. Two implications: (a) treat any crowned active strategy as *time-decaying*, not permanent — a reason our forward paper-trade should keep re-checking vs buy-and-hold rather than trusting a one-time crown; (b) reinforces honesty in the product: the realistic expectation is benchmark-matching, not alpha. Also a data-pipeline note: the "performative" point is a real form of feedback contamination, though it bites large players, not a single paper-sim retail account.

### [16] Confronting Machine Learning With Financial Research
- **Authors / Venue:** Kristof Lommers, Ouns El Harzli, Jack Kim / arXiv (q-fin)
- **Year:** 2021
- **Source:** arXiv:2103.00366
- **% read:** 40%
- **Summary:** Argues ML algorithms were designed for data environments that differ fundamentally from financial markets, and catalogs the finance-specific data obstacles: non-stationarity (regime shifts), low signal-to-noise ratio, **limited *effective* sample size despite large raw datasets** (autocorrelation means N rows ≠ N independent observations), curse of dimensionality, severe overfitting risk, and prevalence of spurious correlations. Also reinforces: don't data-mine indicators, prefer hypothesis-driven strategies; expect spurious "winners"; regime non-stationarity means a window-fit edge may not generalize forward (ties to our forward-paper-trade re-validation). (2) The **deterministic checklist** (zero/negative prices, single source, merge same-timestamp, then robust filter) is a clean template even at daily resolution: reject impossible bars (zero/negative, high<low), fix duplicates, fix a single provenance ([55]), and only then apply a *loose* robust outlier flag for manual review. Note the canonical filter is a **centered** window, which for *cleaning a static historical dataset* is acceptable, but if any cleaning ever feeds a *live/forward* decision it must be made trailing-only to avoid look-ahead ([2][20]). Good source for a "data-cleaning runbook."

### [65] Diffusion-TS: Interpretable Diffusion for General Time Series Generation
- **Authors / Venue:** Xinyu Yuan, Yan Qiao (Hefei Univ. of Technology) / ICLR 2024 (arXiv preprint)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2403.01742
- **% read:** 35%
- **Summary:** A diffusion model for time-series generation that builds **interpretability into the generator** via an encoder-decoder transformer with explicit **trend + seasonality decomposition** (plus a Fourier-based loss), and — unusually — reconstructs the clean sample directly at each step rather than predicting noise. The decomposition lets the model "capture the semantic meaning of the time series" while the transformer handles fine sequential detail. It extends to **conditional generation (forecasting, imputation) and irregular series without architectural changes**, and reports state-of-the-art generation quality vs GAN/VAE baselines on standard discriminative/predictive-score benchmarks.
- **Relevance to our system:** Background (general-purpose, multivariate, not crypto-specific), but it represents the *current* state of the synthetic-data frontier and adds two notes to our standing position. (1) **Interpretable decomposition (trend/seasonal) is a step toward controllable stress paths** — if we ever wanted a counterfactual ("this coin's trend but a harsher drawdown"), a decomposition-aware generator is more steerable than a black-box GAN, and diffusion is more training-stable ([11]). (2) Its **conditional forecasting/imputation** ability connects to the imputation caution [35] — but the same rule applies: any generative imputation/forecast must be causal and is not a "real observation" for the gate. The decisive caveat is unchanged from [4][30][44][50]: these benchmarks reward *general distributional fidelity*, not crypto **jump/tail** preservation under stress, and a generator validated on UCR/energy datasets says little about whether it captures a specific coin's crash dynamics. Net: diffusion (esp. interpretable variants) is the *least-bad* generator family if we are ever forced off real data, but the **moving-block bootstrap remains the default** — it preserves real jumps/tails by construction, needs no training, and is fully reproducible.
