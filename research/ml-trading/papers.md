### [1] Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning
- **Authors / Venue:** Shihao Gu, Bryan Kelly, Dacheng Xiu — The Review of Financial Studies 33(5), 2223–2273 (also NBER WP 25398)
- **Year:** 2020
- **Source:** https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ML.pdf (DOI:10.1093/rfs/hhaa009)
- **% read:** 60% (full PDF fetched; read abstract, method taxonomy, CV scheme, headline results & portfolio section; skimmed appendices)
- **Summary:** The canonical "ML for cross-sectional return prediction" benchmark. Compares OLS (+ generalized linear with penalties), elastic net / LASSO / ridge, PCR, PLS, gradient-boosted regression trees, random forests, and shallow neural nets (NN1–NN5) on ~30,000 US stocks, 1957–2016, using ~94 firm characteristics × 8 macro series + interactions (~900+ features). Uses a strict, expanding-window train/validation/test split (no shuffling) for honest out-of-sample evaluation. Headline: monthly out-of-sample R² is tiny but positive for the best methods (~0.40% at stock level; ~1–2% at portfolio level) while OLS-on-all-features is *negative*. Trees and shallow NNs win; their edge comes from nonlinear interactions linear models miss. A value-weighted long–short decile spread built on NN forecasts earns the highest Sharpe (~1.3+ before costs). All methods agree the dominant signals are price-trend/momentum, liquidity, and volatility.
- **Relevance to our system:** Foundational but cautionary. (1) Even with 900+ features and the best ML, equity-return R² is ~0.4%/month — predictability is real but minuscule, and the economic gain comes from a *huge cross-section of long–short bets*, which a single-coin long-only paper advisor cannot replicate. (2) Validates our skepticism: the gains are concentrated and fragile. (3) The strict expanding-window split with a held-out validation set for hyperparameter tuning is exactly the test-data discipline we must use — never random K-fold on time series. (4) The "dominant signals = momentum/volatility/liquidity" finding is a sanity check on which features are worth engineering at all.

### [2] Advances in Financial Machine Learning (the financial-ML pipeline)
- **Authors / Venue:** Marcos López de Prado — Wiley (book; the de-facto methodology canon)
- **Year:** 2018
- **Source:** https://gildan-bonus-content.s3.amazonaws.com/GIL2476_AdvancesFinancial/GIL2476_AdvancesFinancial_BonusPDF.pdf (bonus PDF) + study notes https://reasonabledeviations.com/notes/adv_fin_ml/
- **% read:** 50% (read structured chapter notes covering the 7 core techniques + bonus PDF; not the full book)
- **Summary:** The standard "how to do financial ML without fooling yourself" reference. Core techniques: (a) **Fractional differentiation** — difference a price series with a non-integer order d (0<d<1), chosen as the minimum d that passes a stationarity test (ADF), to make it stationary while *retaining memory*, resolving the stationarity-vs-memory trade-off that integer differencing destroys. (b) **Triple-barrier labeling** — label each event by which of {profit-take, stop-loss, time-expiry} barrier is hit first, capturing path-dependence that fixed-horizon return labels ignore. (c) **Meta-labeling** — separate *side* (a primary high-recall model) from *size/whether-to-bet* (a secondary binary classifier that filters false positives and sizes bets), improving precision and Sharpe. (d) **Sample uniqueness / sequential bootstrap** — overlapping labels break IID; downweight non-unique samples and bag with `max_samples = avg uniqueness`. (e) **Purged k-fold CV with embargo** — remove training observations whose label windows overlap the test fold (purge) plus an embargo gap afterward, because naive k-fold leaks and "vastly over-inflates" results. (f) **Feature importance** — MDI (in-sample, tree-only, penalizes collinearity), MDA (permutation, out-of-sample, model-agnostic), SFI (single-feature, no interactions); use PCA cross-check for substitution effects. (g) **Deflated Sharpe ratio** — penalize the observed SR by the number of trials run, because selection across many backtests inflates the best SR.
- **Relevance to our system:** This is the single most directly applicable reference for our advisor's *robustness/honesty* layer. Triple-barrier labeling maps cleanly onto our take-profit / stop-loss / max-holding paper-trade mechanics and is a more realistic label than fixed-horizon return. Purged-CV-with-embargo and the deflated Sharpe are the gold standard for the discipline our FROZEN robustness gate already aims at (1000-path bootstrap, weakest-link) — the deflated-SR idea is essentially "punish for the number of strategies you baked off", which is exactly what a multi-strategy bake-off must do to avoid crowning a lucky winner. Sample-uniqueness weighting matters if we ever train a model on overlapping windows. Meta-labeling is a candidate enhancement: keep our simple strategy as the "side", add a small classifier that decides *whether to act* — but only if it beats buy-and-hold net of costs on purged CV.

### [3] Supervised Autoencoders with Fractionally Differentiated Features and Triple-Barrier Labelling Enhance Predictions on Noisy Data
- **Authors / Venue:** Bartosz Bieganowski, Robert Ślepaczuk — University of Warsaw (arXiv preprint)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2411.12753 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12753v2)
- **% read:** 65% (HTML full text; read setup, frac-diff/triple-barrier method, results tables, and the explicit caveats section)
- **Summary:** A direct crypto application of two López de Prado pipeline pieces. On 1-minute OHLC for BTC/ETH/LTC (Binance, 2016–2022; train pre-2020, OOS Jan-2020–Apr-2022), they make features stationary via fractional differentiation (per-feature optimal d chosen by ADF at 1%), label with the triple-barrier method, and feed a supervised autoencoder + MLP. They introduce a labeling-quality optimization metric Φ rewarding correct directional entries. Result: strategies beat buy-and-hold on *risk-adjusted* terms — e.g. BTC 30-min: information ratio ~2.03 at 21% vol vs B&H 63% vol; portfolio 30-min IR 2.56 vs B&H's far higher vol — with transaction costs of 0.05%/side included. But absolute returns were usually *below* buy-and-hold (e.g. BTC SAE far under B&H's 406%).
- **Relevance to our system:** Strong methodological template, weak as an alpha claim. (1) Concretely demonstrates the frac-diff + triple-barrier + walk-forward pipeline on crypto minute data — close to our domain. (2) The honesty caveats are exactly the failure modes our robustness gate exists to catch: the authors themselves flag that the OOS window "remained a highly bullish time", returns across strategies are "highly correlated", and no slippage is modeled. (3) Crucially, the win is *only* on risk-adjusted ratios, not absolute return — consistent with our validated thesis that beating buy-and-hold on total return net of costs is the hard part. For our advisor this means: if we ever report a strategy as a "winner," we must distinguish "lower-vol path" from "more money than holding," and stress-test outside a bull window. (4) The supervised-autoencoder model itself is a deep-learning artifact (cross-ref deep-learning topic); the transferable lesson here is the *feature/label engineering*, not the net.

### [4] XGBoost Forecasting of NEPSE Index Log Returns with Walk-Forward Validation
- **Authors / Venue:** Sahaj Raj Malla, Shreeyash Kayastha, Rumi Suwal, Harish Chandra Bhandari, Rajendra Adhikari — arXiv preprint
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2601.08896 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08896v1)
- **% read:** 70% (HTML full text; read setup, feature list, walk-forward scheme, results tables, limitations)
- **Summary:** A tidy, honest classical-ML forecasting study. Predicts daily log returns of the Nepal Stock Exchange index with XGBoost, using lagged log-returns (L∈{10,20,30}) plus a handful of technical features (5-/20-day rolling vol, RSI-14, 10-day rolling mean). Uses proper walk-forward validation (both expanding and 800-obs rolling windows), refitting each step. Best config (expanding, 20 lags) reaches OOS RMSE 0.0135, MAE 0.0098, R²=0.206, directional accuracy 65.15%, beating Ridge (54.6% DA) and ARIMA (55.3% DA) by ~11–12% RMSE. Top features: 10-day mean, RSI, lag-1, lag-19, short-term vol. Authors note reliance on "historical continuity" fails during black-swan events.
- **Relevance to our system:** Useful as both a positive template and a cautionary tale. Positive: walk-forward (no look-ahead), simple interpretable features, and tree-method-beats-linear is consistent with Gu–Kelly–Xiu [1]. Cautionary — and this is the key takeaway: **the paper reports impressive statistical metrics (R²=0.21, 65% DA) but never benchmarks against buy-and-hold or a random walk, and runs no transaction-cost trading backtest.** This is precisely the gap our advisor is designed to close — a high directional accuracy on log returns says nothing about whether you'd beat holding net of fees, especially since the easiest-to-predict component is often the autocorrelated near-zero center of the return distribution. An R²=0.21 on a single index's returns should be treated with suspicion (likely regime/overlap leakage or an easy bull sample) until shown to survive a costed, B&H-benchmarked, bootstrapped test. For us: never accept directional accuracy as evidence of edge — route it through the FROZEN gate.

### [5] Predicting the Direction of Stock Market Prices Using Random Forest
- **Authors / Venue:** Luckyson Khaidem, Snehanshu Saha, Sudeepa Roy Dey — arXiv preprint (PESIT, Bangalore)
- **Year:** 2016
- **Source:** arXiv:1605.00003 (full text: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/abs/1605.00003)
- **% read:** 70% (ar5iv full text; read labeling formula, feature list, and the accuracy tables — this is the load-bearing part)
- **Summary:** Frames stock-direction prediction as binary classification with a random forest on technical-indicator features (RSI, stochastic oscillator, Williams %R, MACD, etc.). Reports headline accuracies of 86.8% (30-day), 90.6% (60-day), 94.0% (90-day) on Samsung, with 84–95% across Apple/GE. BUT the methodology has two fatal, instructive flaws: (1) the label is `target_i = Sign(close_{i+d} − close_i)` for d = 30/60/90 days — i.e. predicting the *long-horizon* direction, which is far easier and far less tradable than next-bar direction; and (2) they exponentially smooth the *entire* price series first and extract both features AND (implicitly) the trend the label tracks from that smoothed series, which leaks future information backward (the smoothing window straddles the prediction boundary). The "accuracy rises monotonically with horizon" pattern is the tell: a genuinely hard problem does not get *easier* further out — it's smoothing leakage.
- **Relevance to our system:** A canonical "how to fool yourself with ML in trading" exhibit, and a direct argument for our discipline. It teaches three concrete guardrails our advisor already needs: (a) **never apply any transform (smoothing, scaling, frac-diff d-selection, normalization) across the full series before the train/test split** — fit it on train only, then apply forward; (b) **be deeply suspicious of accuracy that grows with horizon or of any direction-accuracy >~55–60% on liquid assets**; (c) **direction accuracy ≠ profit** — a 94% hit rate on 90-day sign with no costed backtest vs buy-and-hold is meaningless for whether you'd beat holding. This paper is the negative control our robustness thesis predicts. Pair with [4] (no B&H benchmark) as the two most common ML-trading sins.

### [6] Downside Risk Reduction Using Regime-Switching Signals: A Statistical Jump Model Approach
- **Authors / Venue:** Yizhan Shu, Chenyu Yu, John M. Mulvey — arXiv preprint (Princeton ORFE)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2402.05272 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05272v1)
- **% read:** 65% (HTML full text; read model definition, OOS protocol, and the headline comparison tables)
- **Summary:** Introduces the **statistical jump model (JM)** for market-regime detection — an unsupervised method that fits regime parameters while adding an explicit **jump penalty λ** that discourages frequent regime switches, fixing the HMM's tendency to produce excessive, non-persistent regime flips. Tested on S&P 500/DJIA/Nasdaq-100 daily data (1990–2023) with a binary 0%/100% in-market allocation driven by a volatility-regime forecast, **net of 10 bp/one-way costs**. Headline (S&P 500): JM annualized return 12.55% vs B&H 9.82% vs HMM 7.51%; Sharpe 0.78 vs 0.46 vs 0.51; max drawdown −24.5% vs −55.2% (B&H) vs −19.0% (HMM). The JM beats B&H on *both* return and drawdown; the plain HMM does NOT beat B&H on return (7.51% < 9.82%) though it cuts drawdown.
- **Relevance to our system:** Important and double-edged. (1) **A genuine counter-example to a naive "nothing beats holding" reading** — a *persistent* regime-timing rule beat equity B&H on total return net of costs over 33 years. But the honest caveats matter: this is binary in/out timing on a chronically up-drifting *equity index* where the whole edge is sidestepping 2008 and 2020; equities mean-revert and have an equity-risk-premium tailwind that a single crypto coin does not, so this result may not transfer. (2) **The plain HMM underperformed B&H on return** — directly relevant because vanilla HMM regime detection is the obvious thing to add, and here it would have *hurt* total return. The persistence penalty (fewer switches → fewer costs → less whipsaw) is what made the difference; lesson: regime signals are only useful if they're persistent enough that trading costs don't eat the benefit. (3) For our advisor, a regime *feature* (vol-state) is worth testing as an input to position sizing or as a "stay in cash" overlay — but it must clear the FROZEN gate against B&H net of costs, and we should prefer a jump-model-style persistent classifier over a flip-prone HMM. The JM is also attractively simple/interpretable (good for our Rust stack — no deep net needed).

### [7] The Probability of Backtest Overfitting
- **Authors / Venue:** David H. Bailey, Jonathan M. Borwein, Marcos López de Prado, Qiji Jim Zhu — Journal of Computational Finance (also SSRN 2326253)
- **Year:** 2017 (working paper 2013–15)
- **Source:** SSRN:2326253 / https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf (PDF body did not extract cleanly; read via abstract + framework synthesis)
- **% read:** 30% (abstract + method framework from multiple secondary descriptions and prior knowledge; PDF body unreadable via fetch, flagged honestly)
- **Summary:** Defines the **Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)** and gives a practical estimator, **Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation (CSCV)**, that is model-free, non-parametric, and symmetric. Mechanic: build a matrix of per-period performance for the N strategy configurations you tried; split the T time periods into S equal blocks; form all S-choose-(S/2) combinations of blocks as in-sample, the complement as out-of-sample; for each combination pick the strategy that's best in-sample, observe its *rank* out-of-sample, convert to a logit. PBO = the fraction of combinations in which the in-sample-best strategy lands below the median out-of-sample. Key results: hold-out / single train-test splits are unreliable for backtests (they answer the wrong question), and **PBO rises sharply with the number of configurations tried** — with enough trials you can manufacture an impressive in-sample Sharpe that has zero (or negative) expected OOS performance. Companion message ("pseudo-mathematics"): a backtest reported without the number of trials is uninterpretable.
- **Relevance to our system:** This is the theoretical backbone of why our bake-off needs a robustness gate at all, and a concrete tool we could implement. Our advisor bakes off *many* strategies/parameterizations on one (coin, window) and crowns the best — which is *exactly* the multiple-trials setup that inflates Sharpe and produces overfit winners. CSCV is directly implementable in Rust over the per-period returns we already compute: report a PBO number alongside each crowned strategy, and treat high PBO as a veto. This complements our moving-block bootstrap: the bootstrap stresses one strategy's path-robustness; PBO/CSCV stresses *selection* robustness across the whole bake-off menu. Together they cover both failure axes. Strong candidate to fold into the FROZEN gate's reporting. (Cross-ref [2] deflated Sharpe — same authors, same "penalize for number of trials" principle.)

### [8] A Unified Approach to Interpreting Model Predictions (SHAP)
- **Authors / Venue:** Scott M. Lundberg, Su-In Lee — NeurIPS (NIPS) 2017
- **Year:** 2017
- **Source:** arXiv:1705.07874
- **% read:** 30% (abstract + framework via fetch; specifics of properties/estimators from established knowledge of the paper, flagged honestly)
- **Summary:** Foundational interpretability paper. Introduces **SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)**, a unified framework for "additive feature attribution" that subsumes six prior methods (LIME, DeepLIFT, layer-wise relevance propagation, classic Shapley sampling, QII, tree feature attribution). It proves there is a *unique* attribution in this class satisfying three desirable axioms — **local accuracy** (attributions sum to the prediction), **missingness** (absent features get zero attribution), and **consistency** (if a model changes so a feature contributes more, its attribution cannot decrease). Provides practical estimators: model-agnostic **KernelSHAP** and the fast exact **TreeSHAP** for tree ensembles. The values are grounded in cooperative game theory (Shapley values). Known limitation (well established in finance use): with **correlated features**, attributions can be misleading because the method evaluates feature coalitions that include unrealistic off-manifold combinations.
- **Relevance to our system:** Moderately relevant — an *explainability/diagnostic* tool, not an alpha source. If we ever ship a tree-based meta-labeler or regime classifier in the advisor, TreeSHAP is the right way to explain to the operator *why* a strategy fired (e.g. "this 'act' decision was driven 60% by short-term volatility, 25% by momentum"), which fits our human-friendliness and honest-reporting goals. Two cautions land squarely on our domain: (1) financial features are highly correlated (momentum, vol, RSI all co-move), so SHAP attributions here must be read with the correlated-feature caveat and ideally cross-checked with permutation/MDA [2]; (2) interpretability does NOT validate predictive edge — a beautifully explained overfit model is still overfit. Use SHAP to communicate and debug, never as evidence the model beats B&H. Background-leaning but useful if/when we add any learned component.

### [9] Replicating Anomalies
- **Authors / Venue:** Kewei Hou, Chen Xue, Lu Zhang — The Review of Financial Studies 33(5), 2019–2133 (also NBER WP 23394)
- **Year:** 2020
- **Source:** SSRN:2961979 / DOI:10.1093/rfs/hhy131 (SSRN body blocked 403; logged from abstract + multiple corroborating descriptions)
- **% read:** 25% (abstract + widely-corroborated headline results; full body not retrieved, flagged honestly)
- **Summary:** The landmark "factor zoo" replication study. The authors compile **452** cross-sectional return-predictor "anomalies" from the published literature and attempt to replicate them under a consistent, conservative protocol. Headline: about **65% fail to replicate** at conventional significance (|t| ≥ 1.96), and the failure rate is even higher under stricter thresholds. The decisive methodological choice is using **NYSE breakpoints and value-weighted returns** (and de-emphasizing microcaps), versus the equal-weighted, all-stock specifications common in the original studies — microcaps are ~3% of market cap but ~60% of the stock count, so equal-weighting lets tiny illiquid stocks manufacture "anomalies" that vanish in economically meaningful (value-weighted) terms. Conclusion: most of the factor zoo is the product of data mining / p-hacking and insufficiently conservative test design; the genuinely robust anomalies are a small minority. (Note: later work — Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen 2023, Chen-Zimmermann — argues replication rates are higher with consistent methods; the debate itself reinforces that *methodology choices dominate apparent results*.)
- **Relevance to our system:** A core skepticism anchor for the whole project, even though it's equities. (1) It quantifies our prior: when a literature systematically searches for predictors, the *majority are spurious* once tested conservatively — so the bake-off's job is to be the conservative filter, not the predictor-generator. (2) The microcap/equal-weight lesson has a crypto analogue: results on thin, illiquid alt-coins or in cherry-picked windows are the crypto "microcap anomaly" — our advisor should weight realism (liquidity, costs, value-relevant sizing) and distrust edges that only appear in low-liquidity or short samples. (3) Reinforces that the headline metric must be economically meaningful and cost-aware, not a raw t-stat on a frictionless long–short. (4) Directly motivates PBO/deflated-Sharpe [7][2]: the factor zoo IS backtest overfitting at field scale.

### [10] Taming the Factor Zoo: A Test of New Factors
- **Authors / Venue:** Guanhao Feng, Stefano Giglio, Dacheng Xiu — The Journal of Finance 75(3) (DOI:10.1111/jofi.12883)
- **Year:** 2020
- **Source:** https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/ZOO.pdf
- **% read:** 45% (PDF fetched; read abstract, the double-selection-LASSO method, and the marginal-factor conclusions)
- **Summary:** A rigorous ML-statistics answer to "is this new factor real?" Proposes a **regularized two-pass cross-sectional regression with double-selection LASSO**: to evaluate a newly proposed factor, you don't just test it alone — you LASSO-select the relevant controls from the existing ~150-factor zoo in both the return equation and the factor-exposure equation (double selection avoids omitted-variable and selection bias), then test the new factor's marginal contribution to the SDF. Key finding: once you properly control for what's already known, the **vast majority of newly proposed factors are redundant or insignificant**; only a small handful add genuine marginal explanatory power. Provides a disciplined, multiple-testing-aware protocol for factor "admission."
- **Relevance to our system:** This is the *methodological how-to* companion to [9]'s *what* — and it generalizes directly to our setting. The principle "test the marginal contribution of a new candidate conditional on everything you already have, with regularization to control multiple comparisons" is exactly how our bake-off should think about adding a new strategy or feature: not "does strategy X beat B&H in isolation?" but "does X add anything beyond the strategies/benchmark we already consider, after honestly penalizing the search?" Double-selection LASSO is implementable in Rust (linear, no deep net) and could underpin a principled "is this new strategy worth keeping in the menu?" gate. Strongly reinforces our anti-overfitting posture and pairs with PBO [7] and deflated Sharpe [2].

### [11] Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection
- **Authors / Venue:** Ryan Prescott Adams, David J. C. MacKay — technical report / arXiv (the canonical BOCPD reference)
- **Year:** 2007
- **Source:** arXiv:0710.3742
- **% read:** 45% (abstract + algorithm core via fetch; the run-length recursion is the load-bearing part and was read)
- **Summary:** The foundational online change-point method. Maintains a posterior over the **run length** r_t (time since the last changepoint), updated recursively as each observation arrives via exact message passing: the run length either grows by 1 (no change) or resets to 0 (change), governed by a **hazard function** H(r) that sets the prior changepoint rate, with per-regime predictive likelihoods from a conjugate model (e.g. Gaussian). A changepoint is signaled when posterior mass collapses toward r_t≈0. Strength: exact, online, real-time, no batch reprocessing. Core limitation explicitly noted: **detection latency** — you need enough post-change observations to be confident, so detection lags the true break.
- **Relevance to our system:** The reference algorithm if we ever add a regime/structural-break feature, and importantly a *reality check* on why it's hard to monetize. BOCPD is light, online, and Rust-friendly (recursive, conjugate Gaussian) — a natural fit for flagging volatility/regime shifts on a single coin's stream. BUT the latency caveat is the crux for trading: by the time a crypto crash or regime flip is confidently detected, much of the move has happened, so a change-point "exit" signal is structurally late — echoing the plain-HMM underperformance in [6]. Verdict for us: useful as a *descriptive/diagnostic* overlay (label the operator's chart with detected regimes) and possibly as a risk throttle, but it must clear the FROZEN gate net of costs before any claim that it beats holding; treat its signals as lagging, not predictive.

### [12] Bayesian Autoregressive Online Change-Point Detection with Time-Varying Parameters
- **Authors / Venue:** Ioanna-Yvonni Tsaknaki, Fabrizio Lillo, Piero Mazzarisi — arXiv preprint (Scuola Normale Superiore / Bologna)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2407.16376 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16376v1)
- **% read:** 50% (HTML full text; read the AR + score-driven extension, datasets, and findings)
- **Summary:** Extends BOCPD [11] in two finance-relevant ways: (1) replaces the within-regime i.i.d. assumption with **AR(q)** dynamics, and (2) lets variance/autocorrelation parameters **vary within a regime** via score-driven (GARCH-like) updates, while staying online through conjugate priors. Tested on bee-waggle (ground-truth validation), US GNP growth (recession vs growth regimes), and financial **order-flow** data. Finds regimes of the same nominal "state" are not identical (growth/recession periods have heterogeneous rates), and that modeling regime structure improves order-flow forecasting, which they frame as helping a trader minimize transaction costs. Notably, the paper does NOT quantify detection lag, false-positive rates, or actual trading profitability.
- **Relevance to our system:** A more realistic change-point model for financial data (volatility clusters → AR + time-varying variance is closer to crypto than i.i.d.), and a candidate if a plain BOCPD proves too crude. But its honest gaps are the warning for us: it shows *forecasting* improvement and *transaction-cost framing*, not a costed, benchmarked trading result — the same "statistically nicer ≠ beats holding net of costs" gap seen in [4]. For our advisor, the transferable idea is using a regime/vol-state signal to *reduce trading* (avoid churning in unstable regimes) rather than to time entries — but, as always, gate it against B&H net of costs. Background-to-moderate relevance; mainly informs how a regime feature would be modeled if we build one.

### [13] Methods Matter: A Trading Agent with No Intelligence Routinely Outperforms AI-Based Traders
- **Authors / Venue:** Dave Cliff, Michael Rollins — University of Bristol (arXiv; presented at IEEE SSCI)
- **Year:** 2020
- **Source:** arXiv:2011.14346
- **% read:** 40% (abstract + framing via fetch; the central claim and methodology critique are clear, full experiment tables not read)
- **Summary:** A pointed negative result. The authors take reportedly best-performing public-domain AI/ML trading agents and benchmark them — under *exhaustive* testing (millions of simulated market sessions across wide parameter ranges, vs the few-thousand-session tests in the original papers) — against a deliberately trivial "sub-zero-intelligence" (no AI/ML) trading strategy. - **Relevance to our system:** Among the most on-thesis papers for our project. This is exactly the gap our FROZEN robustness gate (1000-path bootstrap) and a B&H-always benchmark are built to close. Practical lessons: (1) keep buy-and-hold (our "zero-intelligence" equivalent) as the always-on benchmark — it will beat most clever strategies; (2) the *number and diversity of test scenarios* matter as much as the model — under-powered evaluation manufactures false winners; (3) be especially skeptical of any ML strategy we add that beats B&H only in a narrow test set. Strong citation anchor for the project's core skepticism.

### [14] Backtest Overfitting in the Machine Learning Era: A Comparison of Out-of-Sample Testing Methods in a Synthetic Controlled Environment
- **Authors / Venue:** Hamid R. Arian, Daniel Norouzi M., Luis A. Seco — Knowledge-Based Systems (Elsevier) / SSRN
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** SSRN:4686376 / DOI:10.1016/j.knosys.2024.112477 (SSRN PDF body blocked 403; logged from two corroborating search syntheses + abstract)
- **% read:** 25% (abstract + corroborated method/results from search; full body not retrieved, flagged honestly)
- **Summary:** An empirical horse-race of out-of-sample testing methods in a *synthetic controlled environment* where the ground-truth answer (is there real edge?) is known. They simulate markets with Heston stochastic volatility, Merton jump-diffusion, drift-burst, and regime-switching dynamics, then compare K-fold, purged K-fold, walk-forward, and **Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV)** on how well each controls overfitting. The skew/kurtosis correction is especially apt for crypto's fat tails, where raw Sharpe overstates quality. Concretely: add `deflated_sharpe(N, trial_sharpe_variance, skew, kurtosis, T)` to the gate's report; treat DSR<benchmark as a veto. Top-tier actionable. (Same family as [2][7][19].)
