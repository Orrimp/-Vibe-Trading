### [1] Using Genetic Algorithms to Find Technical Trading Rules
- **Authors / Venue:** Franklin Allen, Risto Karjalainen / Journal of Financial Economics 51(2):245-271
- **Year:** 1999
- **Source:** DOI:10.1016/S0304-405X(98)00052-X (JFE 51(2):245-271; RePEc handle eee/jfinec/v51y1999i2p245-271)
- **% read:** 25% (search-result abstract + bibliographic record; full PDF could not be machine-parsed and the paywalled RePEc page has no abstract — % read kept honest)
- **Summary:** The foundational study using genetic programming to *evolve* technical trading rules (as program trees over price/indicator primitives) on daily S&P 500, 1929–1995, with an explicit train / selection / out-of-sample test split designed to fight overfitting. The motivation is itself the data-snooping critique: hand-picked technical rules look profitable precisely *because* they were chosen for past profitability, so the authors let GP search the rule space "optimally, ex ante." Widely cited result: the evolved rules did **not** earn consistent excess returns over buy-and-hold out-of-sample once transaction costs were included (the S&P equity result is the skeptical headline; related FX work found more out-of-sample profit).
- **Relevance to our system:** The 25-year-old direct ancestor of every modern GP/RL "alpha miner" — and its honest conclusion *is our thesis*: a careful evolutionary search over trading rules, properly held out and costed, **fails to robustly beat buy-and-hold on a liquid equity index**. Strongest historical evidence that automated rule discovery is largely industrialized data-snooping. It models the right discipline (train/select/**test** split + costs) that flashy modern papers drop. Validates that our B&H-benchmarked, cost-aware, bootstrapped gate is the correct, time-tested defense; warns that any param/rule sweep we run will tend to manufacture in-sample winners that die out-of-sample.

### [2] Generating Synergistic Formulaic Alpha Collections via Reinforcement Learning (AlphaGen)
- **Authors / Venue:** Shuo Yu, Hongyan Xue, Xiang Ao, et al. / KDD 2023 (Applied Data Science track)
- **Year:** 2023
- **Source:** arXiv:2306.12964 (also arXiv:2401.02710 follow-up); code github.com/RL-MLDM/alphagen
- **% read:** 65% (abstract + method + experiments via HTML)
- **Summary:** Pioneers RL (PPO) to *generate sets of formulaic alphas* as expression trees; the reward optimizes a *synergistic* combined-factor IC (diversity/complementarity, not single-factor IC), so the agent builds a portfolio of factors a downstream model combines. On CSI300 (train 2009-2018, valid 2019, test 2020-2021), reports IC improving ~0.045 → ~0.085 over baselines, via a Top-K/Swap-N long-only strategy.
- **Relevance to our system:** The modern, more rigorous heir to [1] for automated alpha mining — notably it *does* use a temporal train/valid/test split (better than the LLM-agent papers). But the gaps that matter to us remain: **no transaction costs**, **no quantitative buy-and-hold / index outperformance** (only a visual cumulative-return chart vs CSI300), and the metric is IC (correlation), which is **not** PnL-net-of-costs. A doubled IC of 0.085 is still a *weak* correlation that can easily fail to monetize after costs/turnover (Top-K/Swap-N rebalancing is turnover-heavy). For us: even the best-engineered alpha-mining pipeline reports *statistical* signal, not *gate-credible* tradeable edge. IC is necessary, never sufficient — run any such factor through our cost-aware bootstrap-vs-B&H gate.

### [3] 101 Formulaic Alphas
- **Authors / Venue:** Zura Kakushadze / Wilmott Magazine 2016 (+ arXiv)
- **Year:** 2016
- **Source:** arXiv:1601.00991
- **% read:** 45% (abstract + structure + properties discussion)
- **Summary:** Discloses 101 explicit formulaic alpha expressions (as code) representative of those used at WorldQuant. Key reported aggregate properties: short average holding periods (~0.6–6.4 days) and **low average pairwise correlation (~15.9%)**. The paper is a *catalog of weak, short-horizon, low-correlation signals*, framed around their *aggregate/diversification* properties — NOT a claim that any single alpha is strongly profitable. (Note: a web snippet claimed these were "derived via GP"; the paper itself does **not** say that — it presents them as disclosed formulas. Correction logged.)
- **Relevance to our system:** The canonical reference for "what an alpha factor actually is" — and the target of automated GP/RL miners. The honest picture is sobering: industrial alphas are *individually weak*, short-horizon, and only useful *combined across hundreds* with heavy diversification, frequent rebalancing, and (implicitly) institutional-scale low costs. That entire regime is **inapplicable to our single-coin, retail, cost-sensitive advisor** — one asset means no cross-sectional diversification, and short holding periods mean ruinous turnover costs. Strong support for our thesis: the alpha-factor paradigm is a *cross-sectional, institutional* game; for a single coin held by a retail budget it offers little, and buy-and-hold remains the bar.

### [4] Agent-Based Genetic Algorithm for Crypto Trading Strategy Optimization (CGA-Agent)
- **Authors / Venue:** (crypto GA / multi-agent group) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2025
- **Source:** arXiv:2510.07943
- **% read:** 65% (abstract + method + results via HTML)
- **Summary:** A GA where six LLM/agent roles (Analysis, Generate, Evaluate, Choose, Crossover, Mutation) tune the parameters of a dual-RSI scalping strategy, fitness = a weighted blend of 11 metrics (Sharpe, Sortino, …). Tested on BTC/ETH/BNB 5-minute candles over ~252 days (Dec-2024→Sep-2025), vs the *un-optimized* same strategy. Reports huge PnL: ETH +550%, BNB +169%, BTC +29%.
- **Relevance to our system:** A near-perfect case study of what my mandate warns against — and *directly in our asset class* (crypto, BTC/ETH). It has **no held-out test (re-optimizes every 30 days on the same stream), no transaction costs (on 5-minute scalping!), no buy-and-hold benchmark, and zero overfitting discussion.** A +550% scalping return with no costs is not evidence of edge; it is the fingerprint of in-sample over-optimization that would evaporate under our gate. The "agent-based" GA wrapper changes nothing statistically — it just searches the over-fit space more elaborately. The canonical *anti-pattern*: our 1000-path bootstrap-vs-B&H-net-of-costs gate exists precisely to reject results shaped exactly like this one.

### [5] A Novel Approach to Trading-Strategy Parameter Optimization Using Double Out-of-Sample Data and Walk-Forward Techniques
- **Authors / Venue:** (tmr-crypto; GitHub repo wf_optim_crypto_analysis) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2602.10785
- **% read:** 80% (abstract + method + results + limitations via HTML)
- **Summary:** The methodological *antithesis* of [4], on crypto. Splits data into a Global Training period (optimize via walk-forward) and an **Unseen period evaluated exactly once** with pre-selected params (double-out-of-sample, to stop the "test repeatedly on 'OOS' data" contamination). Optimizes EMA-crossover params *and* the walk-forward window lengths (81 window combos × 6 intraday frequencies), with realistic **0.1% transaction costs** throughout. Honest result: in-sample, every config beat B&H; **out-of-sample the strategy merely *matched* B&H (with lower drawdown)**; at 1–30-min frequencies mean Sharpe was *negative*. A bootstrap vs 1000 random EMA parameter sets showed the *optimized* params beat random only **8–13%** of the time. The only win was a *B&H + strategy portfolio* (diversification, −50% drawdown), not strategy superiority.
- **Relevance to our system:** Essentially an external replication of OUR ENTIRE THESIS and gate. It (a) uses a 1000-path bootstrap baseline (our gate), (b) insists on cost-aware, truly-once OOS evaluation, and (c) concludes optimized active params **do not robustly beat buy-and-hold** on crypto and often lose to *random* params — the definitive rebuttal to GA/agent optimizers like [4]. Methodologically a model we can cite: double-out-of-sample + optimize-the-window-too + bootstrap-vs-random as an *overfitting detector*. The "B&H + strategy portfolio reduces drawdown" finding is the one genuinely actionable positive — worth testing as an advisor *risk* feature, not an alpha claim.

### [6] AlphaForge: A Framework to Mine and Dynamically Combine Formulaic Alpha Factors
- **Authors / Venue:** Hao Shi, Weili Song, Xinting Zhang, et al. / AAAI 2025 (also arXiv)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2406.18394
- **% read:** 70% (abstract + method + results via HTML v3)
- **Summary:** A generative-predictive (neural) alpha miner: a generator proposes factors to maximize a surrogate predictor's fitness while a diversity loss keeps them distinct — contrasted against GP, Deep Symbolic Optimization (DSO), and RL miners on CSI300/CSI500. Its headline idea is **dynamic factor combination**: re-fit factor weights *daily* via regression, keeping the top-N by recent IC/ICIR to combat **factor decay** ("congestion, market-style shifts"). Reports higher IC (4.40% vs 2.09% RL) and a 21.68% real-money excess return over 9 months on CSI500.
- **Relevance to our system:** The one genuinely interesting *idea* in the alpha-mining cluster for us is the explicit acknowledgment of **factor decay** and the response of *dynamically re-weighting* — i.e. a crowned strategy's edge is not stationary. That maps onto a real question for our advisor: should a crowned pick be *periodically re-baked* as its edge decays? But the usual caveats stand hard: **no transaction costs, no buy-and-hold/index net comparison**, IC of ~4% is still weak, and the 9-month real-money claim is short and unverified. The decay insight is worth borrowing (periodic re-evaluation cadence); the alpha-mining machinery itself is not gate-credible and remains a cross-sectional, cost-blind exercise inapplicable to single-coin retail.

### [7] NEAT Algorithm-based Stock Trading Strategy with Multiple Technical Indicators Resonance
- **Authors / Venue:** (NEAT trading group) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2025
- **Source:** arXiv:2501.14736
- **% read:** 70% (abstract + method + results via HTML)
- **Summary:** Applies NEAT (neuroevolution — evolving both NN topology and weights) to evolve a trading network over 7 technical indicators ("resonance" across indicators) on 503 S&P 500 constituents, 22 years (1999–2022), vs buy-and-hold. Result: evolved model averages **18.76% return vs B&H's 27.97%** — it *underperforms* B&H by ~9 pts, with somewhat lower volatility and exposure. Authors note unused nodes/connections (evolutionary "bloat").
- **Relevance to our system:** A refreshingly honest *negative* result: a sophisticated neuroevolution approach with multi-indicator inputs **loses to buy-and-hold by ~9 percentage points before transaction costs even enter** — and costs would only widen the gap. The slight volatility reduction is the same "risk-not-alpha" pattern seen in [3][5]. Strong, direct support for our thesis on a 22-year liquid-market sample. Concrete caution about neuroevolution specifically: topology-evolving methods bloat and overfit, and "resonance across many indicators" does not manufacture an edge that survives the simplest benchmark. For us: neuroevolution offers nothing our gate would crown over B&H.

### [8] Navigating the Alpha Jungle: An LLM-Powered MCTS Framework for Formulaic Factor Mining
- **Authors / Venue:** Yu Shi, et al. / AAAI 2026 (also arXiv)
- **Year:** 2025
- **Source:** arXiv:2505.11122
- **% read:** 35% (abstract + method; specific metrics not in excerpt)
- **Summary:** Combines an LLM (to generate/refine *symbolic* alpha formulas) with Monte Carlo Tree Search, where MCTS exploration is **guided by backtest feedback** on each candidate factor, plus a "frequent-subtree-avoidance" mechanism for diversity. Claims superior predictive accuracy + trading performance vs prior GP/RL miners, with formulas "more amenable to human interpretation."
- **Relevance to our system:** The apex example of the data-snooping concern my mandate flags: an MCTS search *optimizing against backtest feedback* over a vast formula space is a maximally efficient multiple-testing machine — and the abstract gives **no held-out test, no multiple-testing correction, no transaction costs, no B&H-net comparison** (the metric is predictive accuracy, not cost-aware PnL). The interpretability gain (readable formulas vs black-box) is the one genuine plus, and matters for *our* narration goal: a crowned strategy's logic in human-readable form aids explanation. But the mining itself is exactly what our gate should expect to reject — "better at finding in-sample formulas faster" is not "found a real edge." Confirms the pattern across [2][6]: GP/RL/MCTS make the *search* fancier without changing the statistics that doom it.

### [9] EFS: Evolutionary Factor Searching for Sparse Portfolio Optimization Using Large Language Models
- **Authors / Venue:** (Chinese A-share factor research group) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2025
- **Source:** arXiv:2507.17211
- **% read:** 55% (abstract + method + skim of results via PDF; some numerics unreadable in compressed PDF)
- **Summary:** An **evolutionary loop where the LLM is the mutation/generation operator**: the LLM proposes candidate factors, they're scored, and the best are iteratively refined for sparse-portfolio construction. Evaluated on Chinese A-shares vs traditional value/momentum/quality factors, equal-weight, and buy-and-hold; claims improved returns and Sharpe.
- **Relevance to our system:** Textbook example of the **industrialized-overfitting risk** my mandate flags. An evolutionary search that iteratively optimizes factors *against the same dataset* by performance feedback is multiple-testing at scale — the search WILL find spurious patterns. The paper shows no clear walk-forward / held-out validation, no transaction-cost modeling, and no multiple-testing correction. This is exactly the class of result our **1000-path moving-block bootstrap vs B&H gate is built to reject** — an in-sample-tuned factor sweep would almost certainly collapse under the weakest-link bootstrap verdict. Using the LLM as the factor-*generator* doesn't change the statistics; it just generates spurious candidates faster. Lesson: if we ever do automated factor/param search, the search budget must be *charged* against significance (deflated Sharpe / PBO) and validated out-of-sample.

### [10] Evolving Financial Trading Strategies with Vectorial Genetic Programming
- **Authors / Venue:** Rui Menoita, Sara Silva / arXiv (Univ. of Lisbon, LASIGE)
- **Year:** 2025
- **Source:** arXiv:2504.05418
- **% read:** 30% (abstract + comparative-findings via abstract page; full PDF would not machine-parse, so deeper numerics kept honestly out)
- **Summary:** Applies **Vectorial Genetic Programming (VGP)** — GP whose primitives operate on time-series *vectors/windows* rather than scalars, so evolved programs can natively express rolling statistics — to evolve trading strategies on three financial instruments across >7 years of data. Introduces two variants: one with complex-number operations and a **strongly-typed VGP**. But for our skeptical lens the gaps are the usual ones: a small instrument count, "profitable strategies were evolved" without a stated cost model or B&H-net comparison, and no visible multiple-testing correction. Take-away: the *representation* improvement (typed, vectorial primitives) is a genuine GP-engineering advance worth noting, but it does not by itself address whether evolved strategies survive a cost-aware, regime-spanning, B&H-benchmarked gate — which remains the open question across this whole topic.

### [12] RiskMiner: Discovering Formulaic Alphas via Risk Seeking Monte Carlo Tree Search
- **Authors / Venue:** Tao Ren, Ruihan Zhou, et al. / ACM ICAIF 2024 (also arXiv)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Source:** arXiv:2402.07080
- **% read:** 70% (abstract + method + experiments + sensitivity discussion via HTML v2)
- **Summary:** Formulates formulaic-alpha mining as a **reward-dense MDP** (intermediate + terminal rewards) solved by **risk-seeking MCTS** — MCTS exploits the discrete solution space's structure while a risk-seeking (upper-α-quantile) policy optimizes best-case rather than average outcomes. On Chinese A-shares (CSI300/CSI500), 13 years daily (train 2010-2019, valid 2020, **test 2021-2022**), it beats Alpha101, GP, AlphaGen, and tree/NN ML on IC/ICIR/RankIC (CSI300 IC 0.0645 vs AlphaGen 0.0604) and reports the best cumulative backtest return over the 2021-2022 bear market. A sensitivity study shows performance *degrades* for risk-seeking level α>0.85 ("local optimum"). **No transaction costs / spread / slippage** modeled, and **no buy-and-hold benchmark** (only active stock-pickers).
- **Relevance to our system:** Another data-snooping-machine refinement (MCTS + a *risk-seeking* policy that explicitly chases best-case tails — exactly the objective that manufactures lucky-looking in-sample formulas). It does have a real held-out test, but the decisive gaps for us persist: **costs absent, B&H absent, metric is IC**, and the headline "wins the 2021-2022 bear market" is a *single regime window* — precisely the favorable-window result our 1000-path bootstrap spanning many regimes is built to discount. The α>0.85 degradation is a useful caution: pushing an optimizer toward tail/best-case performance overfits. Net: confirms the [2][8] pattern — fancier search (MCTS, risk-seeking) raises IC but is never shown to clear a cost-aware, regime-spanning, B&H-net bar. The risk-seeking-tail idea is a *negative* lesson for us: our gate should reward robust central-tendency vs B&H, not best-case tails.

### [13] Bayesian vs. Evolutionary Optimization for Cryptocurrency Perpetual Trading: The Role of Parameter Space Topology
- **Authors / Venue:** Zhivkov, Kandilarov / Mathematics (MDPI) 14(5):761
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** DOI:10.3390/math14050761 (MDPI page 403-blocked to fetch)
- **% read:** 25% (indexed abstract + Scilit record + corroborating search detail; full text not fetched — kept honest)
- **Summary:** First systematic Bayesian-vs-evolutionary optimizer comparison for crypto perpetual-futures trading: Random Sampling vs **TPE** (Tree-structured Parzen Estimator, Bayesian) vs **DE** (Differential Evolution, evolutionary), across 3 strategies × 3 optimizers × 4 pairs (BTC/ETH/INJ/SOL USDT) = 36 factorial cells, **14,400 backtesting trials with walk-forward validation**. Central finding is about *parameter-space topology*: the fraction of "viable" parameter regions varies enormously by strategy×asset (trend-following 4.6% viable on BTC → 82% on ETH, 17.8×; mean-reversion 10.8% on ETH → 92% on SOL) — topology is a *strategy–asset interaction*, not an intrinsic asset property. TPE won 75% of strategy-asset pairs (9/12), reaching 90% of optimal within 13–17% of the trial budget; DE underperformed on mean-reversion (−1% to −8%) but was competitive/better on trend-following (+13% to +37%).
- **Relevance to our system:** Directly in our asset class (crypto perps, BTC/ETH) and methodologically careful (walk-forward, large factorial). Two load-bearing lessons. (1) **Optimizer choice interacts with the parameter-space shape, which itself depends on the (strategy, coin) pair** — strong support for our *per-(coin,window) bake-off* design: there is no globally best optimizer/strategy, so tuning must be re-done per coin. (2) "Viable fraction" is a useful *topology* lens: a strategy with a tiny viable region (BTC trend-following 4.6%) is one where an optimizer is most likely to land on a fragile, overfit point — a caution our gate should treat skeptically. The paper compares *optimizers* (which finds a good config faster), not whether the optimized config *beats buy-and-hold net of costs* — so it informs our *search*, not our *verdict*. TPE's sample-efficiency (90% of optimum in ~15% of trials) is worth noting if we ever replace grid-sweep with Bayesian search in the bake-off.

### [14] MadEvolve: Evolutionary Optimization of Trading Systems with Large Language Models
- **Authors / Venue:** Yurii Kvasiuk, Tianyi Li, Owen Colegrove, Moritz Münchmeyer / arXiv (Univ. of Wisconsin–Madison)
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2605.23007
- **% read:** 75% (abstract + method + results table + the entire overfitting/p-hacking section via HTML v1)
- **Summary:** An **LLM-driven evolutionary loop** (à la FunSearch/AlphaEvolve) that mutates *trading-system code* — proposing code edits via an ensemble of LLMs, evaluating against a fitness function, and curating diversity via **MAP-Elites grids + island models**. Data: **BTCUSD 1-minute OHLCV** (Polygon), chronological train 2022-23, valid 2024, **test 2025** (to Oct-2025). The backtester includes a **calibrated super-linear market-impact penalty** (per-trade displacement ∝ qty^0.5; impact ∝ k^1.5). Primary objective is **impact-adjusted PnL**; also Sharpe/Calmar/win-rate. Across 5 runs, out-of-sample test Sharpe rises from a 3.82 baseline to 4.45–5.65 with PnL multiples 5.8×–25.6×. Critically, the paper *directly confronts p-hacking*: it measures IS→OOS degradation, compares it against Bailey et al. (2014) multiple-testing theory (finds degradation "well below the p-hacking baseline" for algorithmic components), uses scale-invariant metrics (Sharpe/Calmar can't come from mere position-sizing), and a 3-part sizing decomposition showing evolved PnL exceeds counterfactual scaled-baseline on both splits. It is honest that *feature/forecast* evolution (Run 4) "remains a noisy optimization problem par excellence."
- **Relevance to our system:** The most methodologically self-aware evolutionary-trading paper in this set, and *in our asset* (BTC) — a genuine attempt to do evolutionary search *honestly*. Strong reusable practices: (a) chronological train/valid/**test**; (b) **costs as a market-impact penalty inside the fitness function**, not an afterthought; (c) explicitly **benchmarking observed IS→OOS degradation against multiple-testing theory** as an overfitting test; (d) scale-invariant metrics to rule out sizing artifacts. BUT the gap that matters most to *our* thesis is glaring: there is **no buy-and-hold benchmark** — improvements are measured vs the *pre-evolution baseline strategy*, not vs holding BTC. A 5× PnL improvement over a hand-tuned intraday strategy says nothing about whether it beats simply holding the coin over 2022-2025 (a period where B&H BTC itself swung wildly). Also, a baseline *intraday* Sharpe of 3.82 on 1-min bars is already suspiciously high for a cost-aware system. Verdict: borrow the *discipline* (impact-in-fitness, IS-OOS-vs-theory, scale-invariant metrics) — these are exactly the controls our gate embodies — but note the headline "it works" is un-anchored to B&H, the one benchmark our gate refuses to drop. The most useful single import: **degradation-vs-multiple-testing-theory as an explicit overfitting diagnostic** for our bake-off.

### [16] Markowitz-based Cardinality-Constrained Portfolio Selection using Asexual Reproduction Optimization (ARO)
- **Authors / Venue:** Taha Mansouri, Mohammad Reza Sadeghi Moghadam / arXiv
- **Year:** 2021
- **Source:** arXiv:2101.03312

Together [5][30][31] make a strong, citable case for adding a random-strategy null to our robustness gate. Foundational, cheap, high-value.

### [32] Machine Learning-Based Bitcoin Trading Under Transaction Costs: Evidence From Walk-Forward Forecasting
- **Authors / Venue:** Andrei Bysik, Robert Ślepaczuk (University of Warsaw) / arXiv
- **Year:** 2026
- **Source:** arXiv:2606.00060
- **% read:** 75% (abstract + method + results + statistical-inference section via HTML v1)
- **Summary:** Tests whether ML forecasts of **hourly BTC/USDT** returns become profitable *after costs*. Models: XGBoost, LSTM, iTransformer; ~70,000 hourly bars 2018-2026 (Binance), in a **27-fold walk-forward** (12-mo train / 3-mo valid / 3-mo test, advancing 3 mo). Costs: **10 bp per unit turnover**; **buy-and-hold benchmark** included. Findings: gross forecasts are positive, but **naive sign-based strategies fail once 10 bp costs are imposed** (excessive turnover). A **cost-aware execution filter** (trade only when forecast magnitude exceeds a cost-tied threshold) cuts turnover and revives a long-only XGBoost variant to **>65% annualized, Sharpe >1**. Crucially, **paired circular block-bootstrap inference with Holm multiple-testing correction "does not reject" vs buy-and-hold — i.e. NO statistically significant outperformance.**
- **Relevance to our system:** The closest external mirror of *our exact gate* found anywhere in this topic — it independently uses (a) a **buy-and-hold benchmark**, (b) **realistic transaction costs**, (c) **block-bootstrap inference** respecting serial dependence, and (d) **multiple-testing correction (Holm)** for the many configs tried. And with all that rigor on our own asset (BTC), the verdict is *exactly our thesis*: even a strategy with a flashy >65% annualized / Sharpe>1 headline **fails to beat buy-and-hold once you bootstrap and correct for multiple testing.** This is gate-credible, recent, BTC-specific confirmation that "no active strategy robustly beats holding, net of costs." Two directly actionable imports: (1) the **cost-aware execution filter** (only trade when expected move clears the cost threshold) is a concrete, sensible turnover-reduction mechanism worth testing as a strategy modifier — it's the one thing that made the strategy even *gross*-viable; (2) **Holm-corrected block-bootstrap-vs-B&H** is precisely the statistical posture our gate embodies, and this paper is strong external validation that it's the right bar. The headline lesson: rigor *reveals* the null result that lax papers ([4][20]) hide. Best single capstone for the evolution/optimization-on-crypto cluster.
